In Great Britain, every vehicle over **3 years old** must undergo an annual **MOT (Ministry of Transport) Test** to ensure it meets statutory environmental and road safety standards set by the **Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA)**.

The DVSA sets a **statutory maximum fee cap** that MOT testing stations can charge for an MOT test. However, garages frequently offer discounted MOT test prices (e.g. £35–£45) when combined with annual vehicle servicing.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **DVSA Statutory Maximum Fee Caps (2026/27)**:
  - **Class 4 (Standard Cars, Taxis, Small Vans up to 3,000kg)**: **£54.85 Maximum**.
  - **Class 7 (Goods Commercial Vehicles 3,000kg – 3,500kg)**: **£58.60 Maximum**.
  - **Class 1 & 2 (Motorcycles / Motorbikes)**: **£29.65 to £37.80 Maximum**.
- **DVSA MOT Retest Rules**:
  - **Repaired at Test Station within 10 Working Days**: **100% FREE Retest**.
  - **Taken Away & Returned by End of Next Working Day**: **FREE Partial Retest** for specific items (e.g., wipers, lamps, mirrors, bonnet, doors, wheels).
  - **Taken Away & Returned Within 10 Working Days**: Reduced partial retest fee (up to half the initial test fee).
  - **Returned After 10 Working Days**: Full MOT test fee must be paid again.
- **Driving a Failed Vehicle**: If your car receives a **Dangerous** defect rating, it is a criminal offence to drive it on public roads even if your previous MOT certificate has not yet expired.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Driving a Car Marked with 'Dangerous' Defects**: Driving a vehicle after an MOT failure marked with a "Dangerous" defect incurs a fine up to **£2,500** and **3 penalty points**.
- **Missing the 10-Working-Day Retest Window**: Bringing your repaired car back on day 11 requires paying for a full MOT test all over again.
- **Ignoring Minor Advisory Items**: Advisory items (e.g. slightly worn tyres or brake pads) do not fail the MOT today, but will cause a failure within 3 to 6 months if left unmonitored.
