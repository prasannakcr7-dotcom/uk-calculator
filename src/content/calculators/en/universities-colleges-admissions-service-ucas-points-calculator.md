The **UCAS Tariff** allocates numerical points to post-16 qualifications achieved by students in the UK (such as A-Levels, T-Levels, BTEC Nationals, EPQ, and Scottish Highers).

Many UK universities express their undergraduate entry requirements as a total **UCAS Tariff score** (for example, demanding **120 UCAS Tariff points** for admission onto a degree course). Calculating your combined UCAS points allows you to verify whether your predicted or achieved grades meet university entry criteria.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Official A-Level UCAS Tariff Points**:
  - **A\***: **56 Points**
  - **A**: **48 Points**
  - **B**: **40 Points**
  - **C**: **32 Points**
  - **D**: **24 Points**
  - **E**: **16 Points**
- **Extended Project Qualification (EPQ) Points**:
  - A\* (28 pts), A (24 pts), B (20 pts), C (16 pts), D (12 pts), E (8 pts).
- **BTEC National Extended Diploma (Triple Unit) Points**:
  - D\*D\*D\* (168 pts), D\*D\*D (160 pts), DDD (144 pts), DDM (128 pts), DMM (112 pts), MMM (96 pts).
- **T-Level Distinction\* (168 pts), Distinction (144 pts), Merit (120 pts), Pass (96 pts)**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Assuming All Universities Use UCAS Tariff Points**: Selective Russell Group universities (e.g. Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, LSE) state entry requirements as specific letter grades (e.g. A\*AA) rather than tariff points.
- **Double Counting Overlapping Subjects**: Universities will not accept UCAS points from two qualifications in the same subject (e.g. A-Level Maths and BTEC Applied Maths).
- **Counting AS-Levels Alongside Completed A-Levels**: AS-Level points cannot be added to an A-Level score in the exact same subject.

      <p>The UCAS Tariff is a points system used by UK universities to measure post-16 qualifications. It assigns a numerical score to grades achieved in A-Levels, BTECs, T-Levels, and EPQs, helping universities set course entry requirements.</p>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How many UCAS points is an A-Level grade A worth?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Under official UCAS Tariff rules, an A-Level grade A is worth 48 UCAS points. Grade A* is worth 56 points, grade B is worth 40 points, grade C is worth 32 points, grade D is 24 points, and grade E is 16 points.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Do all UK universities use UCAS points for entry offers?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Approximately one-third of UK universities use UCAS points tariff offers (e.g. "112 points required"). Other institutions (especially competitive research universities) specify exact grade requirements, such as AAB or ABB.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can an EPQ boost my total UCAS points?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Completing an Extended Project Qualification (EPQ) awards up to 28 additional UCAS points (Grade A* = 28 pts, A = 24 pts, B = 20 pts). Many UK universities also lower their standard A-Level offer by one grade if you achieve a high EPQ grade.</p>
    </div>
