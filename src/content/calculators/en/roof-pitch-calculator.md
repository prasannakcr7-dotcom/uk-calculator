Calculating your **roof pitch angle in degrees (°)** and exact **timber rafter length** is essential when designing roof extensions, selecting roof tiles, or converting loft spaces in the UK.

Every roofing material—from **natural Welsh slate** and **plain clay tiles** to **interlocking concrete tiles**—has a strict statutory **minimum pitch limit** governed by **BS 5534** to prevent wind-driven rain penetration.

## ⚙️ Statutory Roof Geometry Formulas & Pitch Rules for 2026/27

### 1. The Roof Pitch & Rafter Trigonometry Formulas
- **Roof Pitch Angle (°)**: `Pitch (°) = arctan(Rise ÷ Run) × (180 ÷ π)`.
  - `Rise` = Vertical height from wall plate to ridge apex.
  - `Run` = Horizontal distance from wall plate edge to center ridge line.
- **Rafter Length (m)**: `Rafter Length = √(Rise² + Run²)`.
- *Example*: A roof with a **2.0 metre Rise** and **3.5 metre Run**:
  - `Pitch = arctan(2.0 ÷ 3.5) = arctan(0.5714) = 29.7°`.
  - `Rafter Length = √(2.0² + 3.5²) = √(4.0 + 12.25) = √16.25 = 4.03 metres`.

### 2. Roofing Material Minimum Pitch Specifications (BS 5534)
Matching your calculated pitch angle to the correct covering prevents water ingress:
- **Flat Roofs (0° to 10°)**: Requires asphalt, EPDM rubber membrane, GRP fibreglass, or torch-on felt.
- **Low Pitch Roofs (12.5° to 17.5°)**: Requires specialized low-pitch interlocking concrete tiles (such as Marley Monarch or Redland Regent) with increased tile headlap.
- **Standard Pitch Roofs (22.5° to 35.0°)**: Standard interlocking concrete and clay tiles.
- **Steep Pitch Roofs (35.0° to 45.0°+)**: Natural slates (minimum 20° to 30°) and traditional small plain clay tiles (minimum 35° or 40°).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Roof Pitch Warnings

1. **Laying Plain Clay Tiles Below 35° Pitch**: Plain clay tiles (small 265×165mm tiles) rely on gravity and steep pitch to shed water. Laying plain clay tiles below 35° causes capillary draw, leading to rotted roof battens and internal ceiling leaks.
2. **Confusing Span with Run**: `Span` is the full building width from outer wall to outer wall. `Run` is half the total span for a symmetrical gabled roof. Ensure you enter the single rafter horizontal `Run` distance.
3. **Decreasing Tile Headlap on Low Pitches**: Lower roof pitch angles require increasing the overlap between tile courses (**headlap**) from 75mm up to 100mm to resist wind-driven rain penetration.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between Roof Pitch in degrees and Roof Slope ratio? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Roof pitch in degrees measures the angle relative to horizontal (e.g. 30°). Roof slope ratio expresses Rise over Run (e.g. 1:2 slope means 1 unit vertical rise for every 2 units horizontal run).</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the minimum pitch for natural Welsh slates? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Standard natural slates require a minimum pitch of **20° in sheltered coastal locations** or **30° in exposed high-wind regions** of the UK under BS 5534 rules.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How does roof pitch affect attic loft conversion head height? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Loft conversions require a minimum finished ceiling height of **2.2 metres**. Steeper roof pitches (35° to 45°) provide generous loft head height, whereas low pitches (<30°) often require a rear dormer extension.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can solar panels be installed on low-pitch roofs? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Solar PV panels perform best at 30° to 40° pitch angles, but angled mounting frames can tilt solar panels on flat or low-pitch roofs to optimize solar generation.</p>
    </div>
