The Scottish Child Payment is a unique devolved benefit available exclusively to low-income families living in Scotland. Administered by **Social Security Scotland**, it provides direct financial support to help parents and carers with child-rearing costs.

Unlike Universal Credit child elements, there is **no limit on the number of children** you can claim for under the Scottish Child Payment.

## ⚙️ Statutory Payment Rates & Eligibility Criteria for 2026/27

### 1. Statutory Payment Rate
- **Weekly Rate**: **£26.70 per week** per eligible child under 16 years of age.
- **Payment Frequency**: Paid into your bank account every 4 weeks (**£106.80 per child every 4 weeks**).
- **Annual Value**: Worth **£1,388.40 per child per year**.

### 2. Qualifying Benefits
To qualify for the Scottish Child Payment, you must live in Scotland and receive one of the following qualifying benefits:
- Universal Credit
- Child Tax Credit
- Income Support
- Income-based Jobseeker’s Allowance (JSA)
- Income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)
- Pension Credit
- Working Tax Credit

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Scottish Benefit Warnings

1. **Assuming Payments Are Automatic with Universal Credit**: The Scottish Child Payment is NOT paid automatically. You must submit a separate application to **Social Security Scotland** online or by phone.
2. **Missing Out on Backdated Payments**: Payments can only be backdated to the date Social Security Scotland receives your application form, so apply immediately upon qualifying for Universal Credit.
3. **Forgetting Other Best Start Grants**: Qualifying for the Scottish Child Payment also makes you eligible for **Best Start Foods** (payment card for healthy food) and **Best Start Grant** lump sums at pregnancy, early learning, and school age.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does the Scottish Child Payment reduce my UK Universal Credit award? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. The Scottish Child Payment is completely ignored as income by the DWP when calculating your Universal Credit or other UK-wide benefits. You keep 100% of both payments.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Is the Scottish Child Payment subject to Income Tax? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. All social security benefits delivered directly by Social Security Scotland, including the Scottish Child Payment, are completely tax-free and do not need to be declared on Self Assessment tax returns.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can both parents claim the Scottish Child Payment if separated? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Only one parent can receive the payment for a child. Social Security Scotland will pay the parent who gets Child Benefit, Universal Credit child element, or Child Tax Credit for that child.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What happens when my child turns 16? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The Scottish Child Payment automatically stops on your child's 16th birthday. However, young people aged 16+ staying in school or training may become eligible for Education Maintenance Allowance (EMA).</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
