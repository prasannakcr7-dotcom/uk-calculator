**Depreciation**—the decline in a vehicle's market value over time—is the single largest hidden cost of car ownership in the UK, typically far exceeding annual spend on fuel, insurance, or road tax.

A brand-new car in the UK loses approximately **15% to 25% of its value in Year 1**, and roughly **50% to 60% of its original list price by Year 3**. Understanding depreciation curves helps buyers select vehicles that hold residual value, compare PCP balloon payments, or decide between buying new versus 3-year-old used cars.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Standard UK Depreciation Curves**:
  - **Brand New Car (Year 1)**: Loses **15% to 25%** as soon as it leaves the showroom forecourt.
  - **Brand New Car (Years 2 to 3)**: Loses **10% to 15% per year**.
  - **3-Year-Old Car (Year 3 Milestone)**: Retains approx. **40% to 50%** of original list price.
  - **Older Used Cars (4+ Years)**: Depreciation flattens to **8% to 10% per year**.
- **Factors Influencing Depreciation Rates**:
  - **Mileage**: High annual mileage (15,000+ miles/year) accelerates depreciation.
  - **Desirability & Brand**: Premium German brands and popular Japanese hybrids retain higher residual values than niche luxury saloons.
  - **Fuel Type**: Rapid EV technology advances have led to faster early depreciation on electric cars compared to petrol models.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Ignoring Depreciation When Comparing Lease Deals**: Focusing solely on low monthly lease payments without evaluating car residual value can lead to expensive balloon payment fees at contract end.
- **Buying Brand New Over 3-Year Nearly New**: Buying a 3-year-old approved used car avoids the heavy ~50% initial depreciation drop, saving thousands in cash loss.
- **Ignoring Colour and Trim Options**: Unpopular vehicle colours (e.g. bright purple) or missing satellite navigation/leather trim can reduce resale values by 10% to 15%.
