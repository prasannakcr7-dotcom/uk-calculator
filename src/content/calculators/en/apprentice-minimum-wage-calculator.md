Apprentices in the UK are entitled to the statutory National Minimum Wage for all working hours, as well as time spent attending off-the-job training at college or university.

Understanding statutory apprentice wage rates—and when an apprentice moves onto full age-bracket National Minimum Wage—ensures fair pay compliance.

## ⚙️ Statutory Apprentice Wage Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Apprentice Minimum Rate
- **Statutory Apprentice Rate (2026/27)**: **£7.55 per hour**.
- **Who Qualifies**:
  - All apprentices aged **under 19**.
  - Apprentices aged **19 or over** during the **first year** of their apprenticeship.

### 2. Transition to Full National Minimum Wage (Year 2+)
If an apprentice is aged **19 or over** and has completed the **first year** of their apprenticeship, they are legally entitled to receive the full National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage for their age group:
- **Aged 21+ (National Living Wage)**: **£12.21 per hour**.
- **Aged 18 to 20**: **£10.00 per hour**.
- **Aged 16 to 17**: **£7.55 per hour**.

### 3. Paid Training Hours
Apprentices must be paid for:
- Normal working hours at their place of employment.
- Mandatory off-the-job training time (at least 20% of total working hours).
- College or university attendance required for the apprenticeship.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Employer Warnings

1. **Failing to Pay for Training Time**: It is illegal for employers to deduct hours spent in college or off-the-job training from an apprentice's pay. All mandatory training hours must be fully paid at the statutory rate.
2. **Keeping Aged 19+ Apprentices on £7.55/hr After Year 1**: Once an apprentice aged 19+ completes their first 12 months, keeping them on the £7.55 apprentice rate is a breach of National Minimum Wage law resulting in HMRC fines and backpay penalties.
3. **Unlawful Deductions for Uniforms or Tools**: Deductions from an apprentice's wages for equipment, uniforms, or training costs that drop their effective hourly rate below £7.55/hr are illegal.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Do apprentices receive paid holiday entitlement? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Apprentices have identical statutory annual leave rights as standard employees—entitled to at least 5.6 weeks (28 days for full-time) paid holiday per year.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can an apprentice work overtime? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes, apprentices can work overtime provided it complies with the Working Time Regulations (maximum 48-hour week). Overtime hours must be paid at least at the statutory minimum rate.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Do apprentices pay Income Tax and National Insurance? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Apprentices are subject to standard PAYE Income Tax and National Insurance rules. However, because annual minimum earnings (£14,722) are near the £12,570 Personal Allowance, tax deductions are minimal.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What happens if an apprentice is made redundant? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Apprentices made redundant after completing at least 75% of their training are supported by the Department for Education (DfE) to complete their apprenticeship assessment with another employer.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
