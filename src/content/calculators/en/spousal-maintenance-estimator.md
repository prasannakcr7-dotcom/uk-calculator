Spousal maintenance (also known as spousal period payments) is regular financial support paid by a higher-earning ex-spouse to a lower-earning ex-spouse following a divorce or civil partnership dissolution.

Unlike **Child Maintenance** (which is calculated using strict statutory CMS formulas), spousal maintenance has no fixed statutory percentage formula under UK law. Instead, courts determine support based on **fairness, financial needs, standard of living during marriage, and the goal of achieving a clean break**.

## ⚙️ How Family Courts Determine Spousal Maintenance

Family judges evaluate financial awards using the statutory factors in **Section 25 of the Matrimonial Causes Act 1973**:
- **Financial Needs & Earning Capacity**: The income, earning capacity, property, and financial resources of each spouse.
- **Standard of Living**: The lifestyle enjoyed by the family before the breakdown of the marriage.
- **Age of Each Spouse & Length of Marriage**: Longer marriages (10+ years) or marriages where one spouse sacrificed a career to care for children typically yield longer maintenance terms.
- **Clean Break Principle**: The statutory mandate requiring courts to consider whether a clean break can be achieved as soon as it is fair and reasonable.

### Common Payment Term Structures
- **Term Maintenance**: Maintenance paid for a fixed period (e.g. 3 to 5 years) to allow the recipient spouse to retrain or adjust financially.
- **Joint Lives Maintenance**: Maintenance that continues until either spouse dies or the recipient remarries (increasingly rare in modern courts).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Remarriage Traps

1. **Automatic Termination Upon Remarriage**: If the spouse receiving spousal maintenance **remarries**, the maintenance order terminates automatically by law. Remarriage completely extinguishes the right to claim ongoing support.
2. **Cohabitation Impact**: Unlike remarriage, cohabitating with a new partner does not automatically terminate a spousal maintenance order. However, it provides grounds for the paying spouse to apply to court to reduce or dismiss payments.
3. **Failing to Register Variations for Change of Income**: If either ex-spouse experiences a substantial change in financial circumstances (e.g. redundancy, severe illness, or major pay rise), you must apply to court to **vary the order**.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Is spousal maintenance subject to Income Tax? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Spousal maintenance is paid out of net taxed income by the paying spouse and is completely tax-free for the receiving ex-spouse. The receiving spouse does not pay income tax on maintenance payments.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between Child Maintenance and Spousal Maintenance? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Child maintenance is paid exclusively for the benefit of children under statutory CMS rules. Spousal maintenance is additional financial support paid directly for the living expenses of the former spouse.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can a spousal maintenance order be capitalized into a lump sum? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. If the paying spouse has sufficient assets (such as equity in a property or cash savings), courts can convert ongoing monthly maintenance into a single upfront lump sum payment to achieve an immediate clean break.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can spousal maintenance payments be increased for inflation? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Spousal maintenance court orders frequently include an index-linking clause (such as annual RPI or CPI adjustments) to ensure the purchasing power of the maintenance keeps pace with inflation.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
