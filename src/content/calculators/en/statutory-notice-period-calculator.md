Under the UK **Employment Rights Act 1996**, employers and employees must give minimum statutory notice when ending an employment contract.

Understanding statutory notice periods ensures that both employers and employees fulfill their legal obligations during contract termination or resignation.

## ⚙️ Statutory Notice Rules under UK Law

### 1. Employer Statutory Notice (Dismissal or Redundancy)
If your employer terminates your contract, statutory minimum notice depends on your length of continuous service:
- **Less than 1 Month Service**: **0 weeks** statutory notice.
- **1 Month to 2 Years Service**: **1 week's notice**.
- **2 Years to 12 Years Service**: **1 week's notice for each complete year of service** (e.g. 6 complete years = 6 weeks' notice).
- **12 Years or More Service**: Capped at **12 weeks' notice**.

### 2. Employee Statutory Notice (Resignation)
If you resign, the statutory minimum notice required by law is **1 week** if you have been continuously employed for 1 month or more.

### 3. Contractual Notice vs. Statutory Minimums
If your written employment contract specifies a **longer notice period** (e.g. 1 month or 3 months), the contractual notice period applies. Employers cannot enforce notice periods shorter than statutory legal minimums.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Legal Warnings

1. **Confusing Statutory Notice with PILON (Pay in Lieu of Notice)**: An employer can require you to stop working immediately if your contract contains a PILON clause. They must pay your full gross salary and benefits for the duration of your notice period.
2. **Gross Misconduct Exceptions**: Statutory notice requirements do **NOT** apply if an employee is dismissed for proven gross misconduct (summary dismissal).
3. **Garden Leave Rules**: During garden leave, you remain an employee receiving full salary and benefits, but are instructed not to attend work or contact clients. You cannot start a new job until your notice period expires.

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Employees are entitled to full contractual pay, annual leave accrual, pension contributions, and benefits (such as private medical or company car) throughout their notice period.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
