Hiring a nanny, housekeeper, or home carer in the UK makes you a statutory **household employer** under UK employment law.

Understanding total household employer costs—including **Employer Class 1 National Insurance (13.8%)** and **3% workplace pension contributions**—ensures complete financial clarity.

## ⚙️ Statutory Household Employer Costs & PAYE Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Employer Class 1 National Insurance (13.8%)
- **Secondary Threshold**: Employer Class 1 NI is charged at **13.8%** on all gross salary paid above **£9,100 per year** (£175/week).
- **Employment Allowance Restriction**: Unlike commercial businesses, households hiring nannies or domestic staff are **NOT eligible** to claim the £10,500 Employment Allowance to offset Employer NI.

### 2. Auto-Enrolment Workplace Pension
If your nanny is aged **between 22 and State Pension age** and earns more than **£10,000 per year** (£192/week):
- You are legally required to enroll them in a compliant workplace pension scheme.
- Statutory Minimums: **3% Employer contribution** + **5% Employee contribution** calculated on qualifying earnings (earnings between £6,240 and £50,270).

### 3. Gross Salary vs. Net Salary Agreements
Never agree a "net salary contract" with a nanny (e.g. agreeing "£500 net per week"). Under net contracts, the household employer is legally liable for unexpected tax code shifts, student loan repayments, and tax increases. **Always agree a fixed gross annual salary**.

## 📊 Practical Nanny Payroll Worked Examples

Below are two worked calculation examples illustrating total household employer costs:

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 1: Hiring a nanny on an agreed gross salary of £30,000/year (with 3% pension)</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Nanny Gross Salary: **£30,000.00 / year**</li>
    <li>Employer Class 1 NI (13.8% above £9,100): (£30,000 - £9,100) × 13.8% = **£2,884.20**</li>
    <li>Employer Pension (3% above £6,240): (£30,000 - £6,240) × 3% = **£713.00**</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Calculation: Total household outlay = £30,000 + £2,884.20 + £713.00 = £33,597.20. Nanny receives £24,119.60 net take-home.</p>
  <div class="net-highlight">Total Annual Cost to Employer: **£33,597.20 / year** (£2,799.77 per month)</div>
</div>

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 2: Hiring a part-time nanny on an agreed gross salary of £12,000/year</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Nanny Gross Salary: **£12,000.00 / year**</li>
    <li>Employer Class 1 NI: (£12,000 - £9,100) × 13.8% = **£400.20**</li>
    <li>Employer Pension: (£12,000 - £6,240) × 3% = **£172.80**</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Calculation: Total household outlay = £12,000 + £400.20 + £172.80 = £12,573.00.</p>
  <div class="net-highlight">Total Annual Cost to Employer: **£12,573.00 / year** (£1,047.75 per month)</div>
</div>

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Nanny Employer Warnings

1. **Self-Employed Nanny Status Myth**: In almost all cases, nannies cannot legally be treated as self-employed. HMRC guidelines dictate that because nannies have fixed working hours, use employer equipment, and work under direct household instruction, they are legally employees subject to PAYE.
2. **Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) & Maternity Pay (SMP)**: Household employers are legally responsible for processing Statutory Sick Pay (£116.75/wk) and Statutory Maternity Pay (£184.03/wk). Small employers can reclaim up to 103% of SMP back from HMRC.
3. **Employer Liability Insurance Requirement**: By law, household employers must hold at least **£5,000,000 of Employer’s Liability Insurance** to cover workplace injuries or accidents.
