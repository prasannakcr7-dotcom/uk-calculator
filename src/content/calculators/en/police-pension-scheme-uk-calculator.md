The **Police Pension Scheme 2015 (PPS 2015)** is a statutory public sector defined benefit pension scheme provided to serving police officers across the 43 Home Office police forces in England and Wales, as well as Police Scotland and the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI).

Under PPS 2015, police officers build up index-linked guaranteed retirement income based on a **Career Average Revalued Earnings (CARE)** structure with an exceptionally generous accrual rate of **1/55.3rd (1.808%)** of pensionable pay for each year of service.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **CARE Pension Accrual Formula**:
  - `Annual Guaranteed Pension Accrued = Gross Pensionable Salary × (1 / 55.3)`.
  - Revalued annually for active serving officers in line with **CPI inflation + 1.25%**.
- **Member Tiered Contribution Rates (2026/27)**:
  - **Up to £27,000**: **12.44%** member contribution rate.
  - **£27,001 to £60,000**: **13.44%** member contribution rate.
  - **Over £60,000**: **13.78%** member contribution rate.
- **Employer Contribution Rate**: Police forces and Police and Crime Commissioners pay an employer contribution rate averaging **31.0%** of officer pensionable pay.
- **Normal Pension Age (NPA)**: **Age 60** for active serving police officers (members can take early retirement with an actuarial reduction from age 55).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing PPS 2015 with Legacy 1987 or 2006 Schemes**: The 1987 scheme allowed retirement after 30 years' service regardless of age; PPS 2015 sets Normal Pension Age at 60.
- **McCloud Remedy Options**: Officers serving between 2015 and 2022 are covered by the McCloud remedy, granting a choice between legacy scheme rules (1987/2006) and PPS 2015 CARE rules for the 7-year remedy period.
- **Opting Out of the Police Pension Scheme**: Opting out forfeits employer pension contributions worth 31.0% of salary, as well as generous death-in-service and ill-health retirement benefits.
