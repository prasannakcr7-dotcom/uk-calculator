Calculating **chronological age**—the precise measurement of time elapsed from a person's date of birth to a specific target date—is required for legal age verification, school admission eligibility, insurance underwriting, medical dosage planning, and state pension age milestones.

While expressing age in completed years is standard in daily conversation, calculating exact chronological age requires breaking down time into **Years, Months, and Days** while adjusting for varying month lengths (28, 30, or 31 days) and leap years (366 days).

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Leap Year Rule**: Leap years occur every 4 years (e.g. 2024, 2028, 2032) adding February 29th (366 total days in the year).
- **Leap Day Birthdays (29 February)**: Under UK law, individuals born on 29 February legally attain their next year of age on **1 March** in non-leap years.
- **UK Legal Age Milestones**:
  - **Age 16**: Can leave school, work full-time, marry with consent (Scotland).
  - **Age 18**: Legal adult status, voting rights, buying alcohol/tobacco, entering legal contracts.
  - **Age 21**: National Minimum Wage adult top rate tier.
  - **Age 66**: UK State Pension Age (rising to 67 between 2026 and 2028).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Dividing Total Days by 365.25**: Simply dividing total days lived by 365.25 gives an approximate decimal year, but fails to provide the exact breakdown of completed calendar months and days.
- **Forgetting Historical Calendar Month Variations**: Subtracting dates across February (28/29 days) vs July (31 days) requires borrowing the exact number of days in the preceding month.
- **Miscalculating Age for Pension or Insurance Eligibility**: Statutory insurance policies and UK pension applications require exact day/month calculations, not rounded year estimates.
