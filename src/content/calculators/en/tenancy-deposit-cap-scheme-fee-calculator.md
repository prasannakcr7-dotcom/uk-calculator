Under the **Tenant Fees Act 2019**, UK landlords and letting agents are legally restricted in the maximum security deposit they can demand from residential tenants.

Understanding the statutory **5-week rent cap** (or **6-week cap for high-rent properties**) and **30-day deposit protection rules** prevents landlords from taking prohibited payments.

## ⚙️ Statutory Deposit Cap Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Statutory Security Deposit Caps (Tenant Fees Act 2019)
The maximum legal security deposit depends on your property's total annual rent:
- **Annual Rent Under £50,000**: Security deposit is capped at a maximum of **5 weeks' rent**.
- **Annual Rent £50,000 or More**: Security deposit is capped at a maximum of **6 weeks' rent**.
- **Calculation Formula**: `Weekly Rent = (Monthly Rent × 12) ÷ 52`. Maximum Deposit = `Weekly Rent × 5` (or `× 6`).

### 2. Refundable Holding Deposit Cap (1 Week Rent)
- Landlords or letting agents can request a refundable **Holding Deposit** to reserve a property while conducting referencing checks.
- Holding deposits are strictly capped at a statutory maximum of **1 week's rent**.

### 3. Mandatory 30-Day Government Protection Rule
Under **Section 213 of the Housing Act 2004**, landlords MUST protect a tenant's deposit in one of three government-approved schemes within **30 calendar days** of receipt:
- Deposit Protection Service (DPS)
- Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS)
- MyDeposits
- **Landlord Penalty**: Failure to protect within 30 days or issue Prescribed Information invalidates any Section 21 eviction notice and exposes the landlord to court fines of **1 to 3 times the deposit amount**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Deposit Protection Warnings

1. **Taking Prohibited Extra Fees as 'Pet Deposits'**: Landlords cannot demand a separate £500 'pet deposit' if the combined total security deposit exceeds the statutory 5-week rent cap.
2. **Failing to Serve Prescribed Information**: Simply transferring cash into DPS or TDS is insufficient. Landlords MUST serve official **Prescribed Information** (scheme terms, tenancy details, dispute procedures) signed by the landlord within 30 days.
3. **Holding Deposit Forfeiture Rules**: Landlords can only retain a 1-week holding deposit if the tenant provides false or misleading referencing information or voluntarily withdraws from the agreement.
