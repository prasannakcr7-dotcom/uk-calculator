In **Northern Ireland**, owning a dog requires obtaining a statutory annual **Dog Licence** from your local district council (e.g. Belfast City Council, Armagh City, Banbridge & Craigavon, Derry City & Strabane).

While compulsory dog licensing was abolished in England, Scotland, and Wales in 1987, Northern Ireland maintains a mandatory licensing system under the **Dogs (Northern Ireland) Order 1983**. It is a criminal offence to keep a dog over 8 weeks old without a valid licence and microchip, punishable by fixed penalty notices of **£80** or court fines up to **£1,000**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Standard Annual Licence Fee**: **£12.50 per dog per year**.
- **Concessionary Annual Licence Fee (£5.00)**: A reduced **£5.00 fee** applies if:
  - The dog owner is **65 years of age or older**.
  - The dog is **neutered or spayed** (veterinary proof required).
  - The owner receives eligible means-tested benefits (Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, Income Support).
- **Free Exemptions (£0.00)**: Assistance guide dogs for visually impaired or hard-of-hearing owners are 100% exempt from licensing fees.
- **Mandatory Microchipping**: Councils will not issue a dog licence unless a valid 15-digit microchip number is supplied at registration.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Failing to Microchip Before Applying**: Submitting a licence application without a valid microchip number results in rejection by council dog wardens.
- **Not Renewing Annually**: Dog licences expire 12 months from the date of issue; failing to renew promptly can result in an £80 fixed penalty fine.
- **Assuming UK-Wide Licensing Rules Apply**: Assuming dog licences are unnecessary because you moved from England or Scotland leads to council enforcement fines; NI law mandates licensing.
