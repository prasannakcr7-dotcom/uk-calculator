Evaluating your personal health and nutrition starts with understanding two key physiological metrics: **Body Mass Index (BMI)** and **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**.

- **Body Mass Index (BMI)**: A statistical measure established by the **NHS** and World Health Organization (WHO) that compares your weight against your height (`Weight in kg / (Height in m)²`) to classify body weight status into standard clinical categories (*Underweight*, *Normal Weight*, *Overweight*, or *Obese*).
- **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**: The minimum number of calories your body burns at complete rest during 24 hours to maintain vital organ functions (heart rate, breathing, cell repair). Combining BMR with your daily physical activity level yields your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **NHS Adult BMI Classification Tiers**:
  - **Underweight**: BMI **under 18.5**.
  - **Normal Healthy Weight**: BMI **18.5 to 24.9**.
  - **Overweight**: BMI **25.0 to 29.9**.
  - **Obese (Class I, II, III)**: BMI **30.0 or higher** (South Asian, Black, and ethnic minority groups use lower risk thresholds starting at 23.0 for overweight and 27.5 for obese).
- **BMR Formula (Mifflin-St Jeor Equation)**:
  - **Men**: `BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in yrs) + 5`.
  - **Women**: `BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in yrs) - 161`.
- **TDEE Maintenance Calories**: `TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier` (1.2 for sedentary to 1.725 for highly active).
- **Weight Loss Calorie Deficit**: Consuming **500 kcal per day below TDEE** creates a 3,500 kcal weekly deficit, resulting in safe, sustainable weight loss of approx. **0.5 kg (1 lb) per week**.

## 📊 Practical Examples

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 1: 30-Year-Old Male (175 cm Height, 75 kg Weight, Lightly Active Exercise)</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Height: 175 cm (1.75 m) | Weight: 75 kg | Age: 30 Years</li>
    <li>BMI Calculation: 75 / (1.75 × 1.75) = **24.5 kg/m²** (🟢 NHS Normal Weight)</li>
    <li>BMR Calculation: (10 × 75) + (6.25 × 175) - (5 × 30) + 5 = **1,699 kcal / day**</li>
    <li>TDEE Maintenance Calories (Lightly Active × 1.375): 1,699 × 1.375 = **2,336 kcal / day**</li>
    <li>Sustainable Weight Loss Target (-500 kcal): **1,836 kcal / day**</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Result: Maintaining 1,836 kcal/day will achieve ~0.5 kg (1 lb) weekly weight loss.</p>

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Applying Standard BMI to Muscular Athletes**: BMI does not distinguish between muscle mass and body fat; muscular weightlifters can register an "overweight" BMI despite having low body fat.
- **Dropping Daily Calories Below BMR**: Eating fewer calories than your BMR (< 1,200 kcal/day) triggers fatigue, muscle loss, and metabolic slowdown.
- **Overestimating Daily Physical Activity**: Selecting "Very Active" when working a desk job overstates daily calorie expenditure, slowing weight loss progress.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is a healthy NHS BMI for adults in the UK?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>For most adults in the UK, a healthy NHS BMI ranges between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m². However, for individuals of South Asian, Black, or Middle Eastern background, NHS guidelines indicate health risks increase at a lower BMI threshold of 23.0 kg/m².</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the baseline calories your body burns at rest just staying alive. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) adds daily movement, walking, working, and exercise calories to BMR to show total daily calorie burn.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How many calories should I eat to lose 1 pound per week?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>To lose 1 pound (0.45 kg) of fat per week, create a daily deficit of 500 calories below your TDEE maintenance level, because 1 pound of body fat contains approximately 3,500 calories of stored energy.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Why does BMR decrease as people get older?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>BMR naturally decreases with age due to sarcopenia (gradual loss of muscle mass) and metabolic rate slowing. Regular resistance training helps preserve lean muscle tissue and maintain a higher BMR as you age.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
