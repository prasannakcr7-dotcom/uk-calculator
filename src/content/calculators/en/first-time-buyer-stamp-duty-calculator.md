First-time home buyers in England and Northern Ireland benefit from statutory **First-Time Buyer Stamp Duty Relief** on residential property purchases.

Understanding the **£300,000 tax-free threshold** and the **£500,000 upper property limit** allows prospective buyers to calculate exact closing costs before exchanging contracts.

## ⚙️ Statutory First-Time Buyer SDLT Rates for 2026/27

### 1. First-Time Buyer Tax Bands (Properties up to £500,000)
- **Up to £300,000**: **0% SDLT** (Tax-Free).
- **£300,001 to £500,000**: **5% SDLT** on the portion within this band.
- *Example*: Purchasing a home for £400,000 incurs 0% tax on the first £300,000 and 5% tax on the remaining £100,000, totaling **£5,000.00 SDLT**.

### 2. The £500,000 Maximum Price Cap
If the property purchase price exceeds **£500,000**, First-Time Buyer Relief is **completely lost**. The transaction is taxed under standard buyer SDLT rates (0% up to £250,000; 5% from £250,001 to £925,000).

### 3. Statutory First-Time Buyer Definition
To qualify for FTB relief:
- You must intend to occupy the property as your **main residence**.
- You must **NEVER have owned or held a major interest in a residential property** anywhere in the UK or abroad (including inherited property or property held in trust).
- For joint purchases, **BOTH buyers must satisfy the first-time buyer test**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Stamp Duty Warnings

1. **Buying Jointly with a Non-First-Time Buyer**: If a first-time buyer purchases a property jointly with a partner who previously owned a property, the entire purchase loses FTB relief and is taxed at standard SDLT rates.
2. **Inherited Property Abroad Disqualifies You**: Having ever inherited a partial share in a family home abroad automatically disqualifies you from UK First-Time Buyer Stamp Duty Relief.
3. **Devolved Nations (Scotland & Wales)**: First-Time Buyer SDLT applies in England and Northern Ireland. Scotland uses **Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT)** with a £175,000 FTB threshold. Wales uses **Land Transaction Tax (LTT)** which offers no dedicated FTB relief.
