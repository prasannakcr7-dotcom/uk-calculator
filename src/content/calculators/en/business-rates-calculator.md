Business rates are non-domestic rates collected by local councils in England, Scotland, and Wales on commercial properties such as shops, offices, warehouses, pubs, and factories.

Understanding how your property's **Rateable Value (RV)** and **Small Business Rate Relief (SBRR)** interact allows commercial occupiers to calculate exact annual business rates bills.

## ⚙️ Statutory Multipliers & SBRR Relief Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Statutory Business Rates Multipliers (England)
Business rates are calculated by multiplying a property's Rateable Value by a statutory poundage multiplier:
- **Small Business Multiplier (RV under £51,000)**: **49.9p** (0.499).
- **Standard Multiplier (RV £51,000 or higher)**: **54.6p** (0.546).

### 2. Small Business Rate Relief (SBRR) Rules
If a commercial property is your **only business property** in England:
- **Rateable Value up to £12,000**: You receive **100% SBRR relief** (**£0.00 business rates payable**).
- **Rateable Value £12,001 to £15,000**: SBRR relief decreases on a linear sliding scale taper from 100% down to 0%.
- **Rateable Value £15,001 to £51,000**: You pay 0% relief, but your bill is calculated using the lower **49.9p Small Business Multiplier**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Commercial Warnings

1. **Losing SBRR by Opening a Second Business Property**: If you take on a second commercial property, you lose 100% SBRR on your main property after 12 months unless the second property has an RV under £2,899 and total combined RV is under £20,000.
2. **Empty Property Rates (EPR)**: After a commercial property becomes vacant, business rates are 100% exempt for the first **3 months** (or 6 months for industrial warehouses). After this period, full 100% business rates become payable.
3. **Retail, Hospitality and Leisure (RHL) Relief**: Eligible high-street shops, cafes, pubs, and hotels may qualify for temporary government RHL percentage discounts on their annual business rates bill.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is a property's Rateable Value and how is it revalued? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Rateable Value is the Valuation Office Agency's (VOA) estimate of a commercial property's open market annual rental value on a statutory valuation date. Revaluations take place every 3 years in the UK.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Are business rates different in Scotland and Wales? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Scotland uses Non-Domestic Rates (NDR) poundage rates and the Small Business Bonus Scheme (SBBS). Wales sets independent Welsh Land and Business Rates multipliers and small business relief tiers.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can a home business claim Small Business Rate Relief? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>In most cases, working from home does not incur business rates if you use a standard bedroom or study. Business rates only apply if a home is substantially converted for commercial use (like a vehicle repair workshop or beauty salon).</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How do I appeal an inaccurate VOA Rateable Value? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>You can check, challenge, and appeal your property's rateable value for free using the VOA's online service on GOV.UK. If the VOA agrees your RV is too high, your business rates bill will be backdated and refunded.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
