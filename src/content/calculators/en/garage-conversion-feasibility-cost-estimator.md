Converting an under-utilized **integral or attached single garage** into a home office, playroom, ground-floor bedroom, or kitchen extension is one of the quickest, highest ROI home improvements in the UK.

Because the main foundation, external walls, and roof structure already exist, garage conversion costs average **£1,000 to £1,450 per square metre**—yielding a **10% to 15% increase in total property market value**.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Garage Conversion Rules & Requirements for 2026/27

### 1. Planning Permission vs Permitted Development
- **Integral / Attached Garages**: Converting an integral or attached garage into habitable living space falls under **Class A Permitted Development (PD)** and generally does **NOT require planning permission** (unless in a conservation area, listed building, or if planning conditions restrict parking).
- **Detached Garages**: Converting a standalone detached garage into an independent annex or granny flat may require full planning permission or a Change of Use application.

### 2. Mandatory Building Regulations Compliance
Even if planning permission is exempt, **Building Control approval is legally mandatory**:
- **Infill Wall Foundation (Part A)**: Removing the main garage door and building a brick/block infill wall with a window requires extending existing foundation footings or inserting a concrete lintel.
- **Damp Proofing (Part C)**: Laying a new Damp Proof Membrane (DPM) over the concrete garage floor slab, connected seamlessly to the walls.
- **Thermal Insulation (Part L)**: Floors require 100mm rigid PIR insulation (e.g. Celotex); external single-skin walls require 100mm studwork insulation achieving a target **0.18 W/m²K U-value**.
- **Ventilation & Electrics (Parts F & P)**: Windows must feature opening trickle vents, and all electrical wiring must be certified under Part P.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Garage Conversion Warnings

1. **Ignoring Concrete Floor Step & Ceiling Height**: Garage floor slabs sit lower than main house floors. Adding 100mm PIR insulation, DPM, and 65mm floor screed raises the new floor level to match the house, reducing finished room ceiling height. Ensure you have at least **2.2m clear height**.
2. **Failing to Inspect Existing Foundation Footings Under the Door**: The concrete slab directly under the old garage door is often thin (~50mm). Building a heavy brick infill wall over a thin slab causes settlement cracking. Dig a small trial pit to verify foundation depth.
3. **Planning Restrictions on Off-Street Parking**: Some local councils attached specific planning conditions when the house was built requiring the garage to be retained for car parking. Check your original house planning consent before starting work.
