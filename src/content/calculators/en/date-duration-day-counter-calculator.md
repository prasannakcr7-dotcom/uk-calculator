Calculating the precise duration between two dates—or determining the exact future calendar date when adding a specific number of days—is a frequent requirement for UK legal notice periods, employment probation deadlines, tax filing cut-offs, tenancy agreements, and project management timelines.

Under UK legal rules (such as Civil Procedure Rules Part 2.8), date calculations can be performed on either a **total calendar day** basis or a **working business day** basis (which excludes weekends and public bank holidays).

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Inclusive vs Exclusive Date Counting**: Standard UK legal procedure excludes the day on which the period begins and includes the final day on which the event occurs.
- **Working Business Days (Monday to Friday)**: Excludes Saturdays and Sundays.
- **Calendar Months vs Days**: Adding 1 calendar month to 31 January lands on 28 February (or 29 February in leap years), rather than 3 March.
- **Statutory UK Tax Year Dates**: The UK personal tax year runs from **6 April to 5 April** inclusive (exactly 365 calendar days).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Forgetting Weekend Adjustments for Notice Periods**: A "14-day working notice" served on a Friday expires 18 calendar days later because it spans two full weekends!
- **Leap Year Miscalculation**: Failing to account for February 29th when calculating multi-year date spans produces a 1-day error.
- **Confusing Working Days with Bank Holidays**: Excluding Saturdays and Sundays accounts for standard weekends, but statutory UK Bank Holidays must also be considered for commercial contract deadlines.
