Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax added to most goods and services sold by registered businesses in the UK.

Understanding how to **add VAT to a net amount** or **extract VAT from a gross total** is essential for business invoicing, expense claiming, and financial management.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK VAT Rates & Registration Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Statutory VAT Rate Tiers
- **Standard Rate (20%)**: Applies to most UK commercial goods and services.
- **Reduced Rate (5%)**: Applies to domestic fuel and power, energy-saving equipment, and children's car seats.
- **Zero Rate (0%)**: Applies to most human food, children's clothing, books, newspapers, and prescription medicines.
- **Exempt Items**: Financial services, postal services, health, and education.

### 2. Mandatory VAT Registration Threshold
Under UK law, a business **MUST register for VAT with HMRC** if its total VAT-taxable turnover exceeds **£90,000** over any rolling 12-month period (or is expected to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days).

## 📊 Practical VAT Worked Examples

Below are two worked calculation examples illustrating adding and extracting VAT:

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 1: Adding 20% VAT to a Net Price of £150.00</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Net Amount: **£150.00**</li>
    <li>VAT Rate: **20% (Standard Rate)**</li>
    <li>VAT Amount: £150.00 × 20% = **£30.00**</li>

  <p>Calculation: Gross Price = £150.00 + £30.00 = £180.00.</p>

</div>

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 2: Extracting 20% VAT from a Gross Price of £240.00</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Gross Amount: **£240.00**</li>
    <li>Net Formula: £240.00 ÷ 1.20 = **£200.00**</li>
    <li>VAT Amount: £240.00 - £200.00 = **£40.00** (or 1/6th of gross)</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Calculation: Net Price = £200.00. VAT Portion = £40.00.</p>
  <div class="net-highlight">Extracted Net Price: **£200.00** (VAT Portion: **£40.00**)</div>
</div>

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & VAT Extraction Errors

1. **The 20% Extraction Error**: A very common mistake is calculating 20% *of the gross price* to find the VAT portion (e.g. 20% of £120 = £24). To extract 20% VAT from a gross price, you must divide by **1.20** (or multiply by **1/6th**).
2. **Confusing Zero-Rated with Exempt Goods**: Zero-rated sales (0%) count toward your £90,000 VAT registration turnover threshold, and allow businesses to reclaim input VAT on expenses. Exempt sales do NOT count toward turnover and do not allow input VAT reclamation.
3. **Failing to Comply with Making Tax Digital (MTD)**: All VAT-registered businesses in the UK are legally required to keep digital accounting records and submit quarterly VAT returns to HMRC using MTD-compatible software.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can a business register for VAT voluntarily below the £90,000 limit? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Businesses turning over under £90,000 can register voluntarily. Voluntary registration is beneficial if you sell primarily to other VAT-registered businesses, allowing you to reclaim input VAT on business purchases.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the Flat Rate VAT Scheme for small businesses? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The Flat Rate Scheme allows small businesses (turnover under £150,000) to pay a fixed flat percentage of gross turnover to HMRC (e.g. 10.5% to 14.5% depending on sector) rather than tracking exact input/output VAT.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Standard Royal Mail postal services and postage stamps are exempt from VAT. However, commercial courier services (such as DPD or DHL) charge standard 20% VAT.</p>
