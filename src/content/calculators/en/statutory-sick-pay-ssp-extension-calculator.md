**Statutory Sick Pay (SSP)** is a mandatory legal payment made by UK employers to eligible employees who are off work due to illness or injury.

Understanding the **£116.75 weekly SSP rate** and the **28-week statutory maximum limit** ensures employees and employers manage sick leave transitions smoothly.

## ⚙️ Statutory SSP Rules & Eligibility for 2026/27

### 1. Statutory Weekly Benefit Rate
- **Weekly SSP Rate**: **£116.75 per week** (£23.35 per working day for a 5-day work week).
- **Lower Earnings Limit (LEL)**: Employees must earn an average of at least **£123.00 per week** before tax to qualify.

### 2. Waiting Days Rule
- SSP is paid from the **4th qualifying day** of sickness. The first 3 consecutive days of absence are un-paid "waiting days" unless linked to a previous sickness spell within 8 weeks.

### 3. 28-Week Statutory Maximum Cap & Form SSP1
- SSP is paid for a statutory maximum limit of **28 weeks (140 working days)** per spell of sickness.
- **Form SSP1**: Once an employee reaches 28 weeks of SSP, the employer MUST issue **Form SSP1**. This form allows the employee to transition from employer SSP to DWP **New Style Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Sick Pay Warnings

1. **Company Sick Pay vs Statutory Sick Pay**: Many UK employers offer contractual **Company Sick Pay (CSP)** (such as full pay for 4 weeks). Contractual CSP satisfies your employer's legal obligation to pay SSP as long as CSP pay is at least £116.75/wk.
2. **Linked Periods of Sickness**: If you are off sick twice within **8 weeks**, DWP treats the absences as a single "linked period of sickness". You do NOT have to repeat the 3 un-paid waiting days for the second absence.
3. **Fit Notes (Doctor Statements)**: Employees can self-certify sickness for the first **7 days**. From day 8 onward, you MUST provide a valid **Fit Note** (Statement of Fitness for Work) from a doctor or healthcare professional to receive SSP.

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Is Statutory Sick Pay taxable under PAYE? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Statutory Sick Pay is treated as standard employment earnings. Employers deduct PAYE Income Tax and Class 1 National Insurance from SSP payments before paying net wages.</p>
