Planning for **care home fees** (residential care or nursing care) is a major financial issue for elderly UK residents and their families. Average UK residential care home fees range from **£800 to £1,200 per week (£40,000 to £60,000 per year)**.

Local authorities conduct a statutory **Financial Assessment (Means Test)** under the **Care Act 2014** to determine whether you qualify for council funding support or must pay 100% of care home fees as a **self-funder**.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Care Home Capital Thresholds for 2026/27 (England)

### 1. Capital Assets Thresholds (Savings, Investments & Property)
- **Upper Capital Limit (£23,250 in England)**:
  - If your total capital assets exceed **£23,250**, you are classed as a **self-funder** and MUST pay **100% of your care home fees** out of your own funds.
- **Between £14,250 and £23,250 (Partial Council Support)**:
  - If your capital is between £14,250 and £23,250, the local council contributes toward care fees, but assumes a **'Tariff Income'** of **£1.00 per week for every £250** of capital held between these two limits.
- **Lower Capital Limit (£14,250 in England)**:
  - If your capital falls below **£14,250**, capital is ignored entirely, and the local council provides **maximum financial support** (you contribute only your weekly income minus the statutory Personal Expenses Allowance).

### 2. Personal Expenses Allowance (PEA)
- Even if a resident's care home fees are funded by the local council, the resident is legally entitled to retain a statutory **Personal Expenses Allowance (PEA)** of **£24.95 per week** from their State/Private pension for personal pocket money (toiletries, clothing, stationery).

### 3. The 12-Week Property Disregard & Partner Rules
- **12-Week Property Disregard**: When moving into a care home permanently, the value of your main home is IGNORED by the local council for the first 12 weeks of care.
- **Partner Protection**: If your spouse, civil partner, or dependent family member aged 60+ continues to live in your main home as their primary residence, the value of the property is **PERMANENTLY DISREGARDED** from the care home means test.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Care Home Warnings

1. **Deprivation of Assets Rule**: Giving away money, transferring property to children, or buying expensive gifts specifically to drop capital below £23,250 to avoid care fees is classed as **Intentional Deprivation of Assets**. Local councils have legal powers to investigate asset transfers with no time limit and charge care fees as if you still owned the assets.
2. **NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC) Fully Funded Care**: If a person's primary need is a complex, unpredictable **medical healthcare need** (e.g. advanced dementia, severe stroke, terminal illness), they may qualify for **100% NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC)** funding. CHC is non-means-tested and pays 100% of care home costs regardless of wealth.
3. **Third-Party Top-Up Fees**: If a council-funded resident chooses a care home that charges more than the council's standard funding rate, a family member must pay a **Third-Party Top-Up Fee** to cover the difference.
