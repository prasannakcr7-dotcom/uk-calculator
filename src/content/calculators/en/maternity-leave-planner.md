Planning maternity leave requires managing crucial statutory deadlines set by UK employment law. All eligible pregnant employees in the UK have the statutory right to **52 weeks of Statutory Maternity Leave**.

Understanding key milestones—such as your **15th week notice deadline** and early leave triggers—ensures you protect your employment rights while preparing for baby's arrival.

## ⚙️ Statutory Maternity Leave Structure & Milestones

### 1. 52-Week Leave Division
Maternity leave is divided into two 26-week blocks:
- **Ordinary Maternity Leave (OML)**: First 26 weeks.
- **Additional Maternity Leave (AML)**: Subsequent 26 weeks.
- **Compulsory Leave**: You **must** take at least 2 weeks of leave immediately after the birth (or 4 weeks if you work in a factory).

### 2. Key Statutory Timelines
- **Earliest Leave Start**: You can choose to start your maternity leave up to **11 weeks before** your expected week of childbirth (EWC).
- **15th Week Qualifying Deadline**: By the 15th week before your due date, you must formally notify your employer in writing of your pregnancy, your due date, and your planned leave start date.
- **Premature Birth / Sickness Triggers**: If your baby is born early or if you are off work with a pregnancy-related illness in the **4 weeks before your due week**, your maternity leave starts automatically on the next day.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Employee Warnings

1. **Missing the 15th Week Statutory Notice Deadline**: Failing to notify your employer in writing by the 15th week before your due week can delay your statutory rights and SMP payments.
2. **Forgetting Holiday Accrual During Leave**: You continue to **accrue full paid annual leave** (including bank holidays) throughout all 52 weeks of maternity leave. Many employees add accrued holiday onto the beginning or end of their leave.
3. **Triggering Leave Early via Sick Leave**: If you take pregnancy-related sick leave during the final 4 weeks before your due date, your employer is legally required to start your maternity leave automatically.

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. If you wish to return to work earlier or later than originally planned, you must give your employer at least 8 weeks' written notice before your new return date.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Do I have the right to return to my same job after maternity leave? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>If you take 26 weeks or less (Ordinary Maternity Leave), you have the absolute legal right to return to the exact same job. If you take up to 52 weeks (Additional Maternity Leave), you have the right to return to the same job unless it is not reasonably practicable, in which case a suitable alternative position with equal pay must be offered.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How do Keep in Touch (KIT) days work during maternity leave? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>You can work up to 10 KIT days during your maternity leave without ending your leave or losing SMP. You and your employer must mutually agree on KIT days, and pay for KIT days is negotiated between you and your employer.</p>
