Buying a used car in the UK—whether from a franchised dealership, an independent car trader, or a private seller on AutoTrader—requires budgeting for more than just the agreed vehicle purchase price.

To avoid hidden financial traps, smart UK car buyers conduct a mandatory **HPI Vehicle History Check** (to verify the car is not stolen, written off, or subject to unpaid hire purchase finance) and arrange a **Pre-Purchase Mechanical Inspection** (via the AA, RAC, or an independent garage).

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Total Buyer Outlay Formula**:
  `Total Outlay = Purchase Price + HPI Check Fee + Mechanical Inspection Fee + Servicing Reserve + Annual Road Tax (VED)`.
- **HPI Check Provenance Verification**:
  - Checks if the vehicle has **outstanding finance** (if bought, the finance company legally owns the car!).
  - Checks if the vehicle is logged as **stolen** on the police National Computer.
  - Checks insurance **write-off categories** (Category N = non-structural damage, Category S = structural damage).
  - Checks **mileage clocking anomalies** against DVLA MOT records.
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015 Rights**:
  - **Dealership Purchase**: You have a statutory **30-day right to reject** a defective car for a 100% refund. Between 30 days and 6 months, the dealer gets one repair attempt before a refund is due.
  - **Private Purchase**: Sold "as seen". No statutory warranty exists; your only protection is ensuring the seller did not misrepresent the vehicle.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Skipping the HPI Check on Private Sales**: If you buy a private car with unpaid PCP finance attached, the finance company can repossess the car from your driveway without compensating you!
- **Ignoring Insurance Write-off History (Cat S/N)**: Category S structural write-offs carry up to 30% lower resale values and are more expensive to insure.
- **Forgetting Immediate Servicing Reserves**: Most used cars sold privately are due for immediate routine servicing, brake fluid changes, or timing belt replacements within 3 months of purchase.

    <div class="faq-content">

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Category N (Non-structural) means the car suffered cosmetic or electrical damage where repair costs exceeded market value, but structural chassis integrity remained intact. Category S (Structural) means the vehicle suffered structural chassis damage that was professionally repaired.</p>
