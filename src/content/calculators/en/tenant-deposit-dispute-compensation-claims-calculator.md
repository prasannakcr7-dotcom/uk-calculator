Under UK housing legislation (Housing Act 2004 and Localism Act 2011), any landlord who receives a cash deposit for an **Assured Shorthold Tenancy (AST)** in England and Wales MUST protect that deposit in a government-approved tenancy deposit protection scheme (**DPS**, **TDS**, or **MyDeposits**) within **30 calendar days** of receiving it.

If a landlord fails to protect the deposit within 30 days, or fails to serve the official **Prescribed Information** to the tenant within 30 days, county courts MUST order the landlord to pay the tenant statutory compensation of between **1x and 3x the deposit amount** (plus returning the original deposit).

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Tenancy Deposit Rules & Penalties for 2026/27

### 1. Landlord Statutory Obligations (The 30-Day Rule)
Within **30 days** of receiving a tenancy deposit, the landlord or letting agent MUST:
- Protect the deposit in a government-backed tenancy deposit scheme (DPS, TDS, or MyDeposits).
- Serve the tenant with the official **Prescribed Information** document (detailing the scheme address, deposit certificate, and terms for deposit deductions).

### 2. Statutory 1x to 3x Penalty Awards (Housing Act 2004 Section 214)
If the landlord breaches the 30-day protection rule, the tenant can file a claim in County Court under Section 214 of the Housing Act 2004:
- **1x Deposit Penalty**: Awarded for minor technical breaches or short delays where the landlord self-corrected.
- **2x Deposit Penalty**: Awarded where the deposit remained unprotected for the majority of the tenancy.
- **3x Deposit Penalty (Maximum Award)**: Awarded for serious breaches, experienced landlords/agents, or where the landlord refused to protect the deposit after being reminded.
- *Total Recovery Formula*: `Total Tenant Award = Original Deposit Refund + ( Deposit Amount × 1x to 3x Penalty Multiplier )`.
- *Example*: A tenant paying a **£1,380 deposit** where the landlord failed to protect the deposit (court awards **2x penalty**):
  - `Original Deposit Refund = £1,380.00`.
  - `2x Statutory Penalty = £1,380 × 2 = £2,760.00`.
  - `Total Financial Recovery = £1,380 + £2,760 = £4,140.00 Total Award`.

### 3. Tenant Fees Act 2019 Deposit Caps
Under the **Tenant Fees Act 2019**:
- For tenancies with an annual rent under £50,000, tenancy deposits are capped at a maximum of **5 weeks' rent**.
- Any deposit taken above 5 weeks' rent is an **illegal prohibited payment**, and landlords can be fined up to £5,000 by local Trading Standards.

### 4. Section 21 Eviction Notice Invalidation
- If a landlord has failed to protect a tenancy deposit within 30 days or serve Prescribed Information, they CANNOT legally serve a valid **Section 21 'no fault' eviction notice** to evict the tenant until they have refunded the full deposit to the tenant.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Tenant Deposit Warnings

1. **Making a Claim After the 6-Year Limitation Period**: Under Section 9 of the Limitation Act 1980, tenants have **6 years** from the date the deposit was paid to submit a Section 214 court claim.
2. **Accepting Unjustified End-of-Tenancy Deductions**: Landlords CANNOT deduct money from deposits for fair wear and tear (e.g. minor scuffs on walls or worn carpets). Deductions are strictly limited to tenant damage or rent arrears.
3. **Submitting a Claim While Still Living in the Property**: While legally permitted, claiming a 3x deposit penalty during an active tenancy may damage landlord relations. Many tenants wait until moving out before launching a claim.
