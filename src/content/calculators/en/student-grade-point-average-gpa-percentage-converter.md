Converting between North American **Grade Point Average (GPA)** on a 4.0 scale and **UK University Degree Classifications** (First Class, 2:1, 2:2, 3rd) is essential for international students applying to UK master's or PhD programmes, as well as UK graduates applying to US corporate graduate schemes.

Because the UK university grading scale ranges from **0% to 100%**—where **70%+ is a First Class Honours**—American students often mistakenly assume a 70% UK mark equates to a US C grade. In reality, a UK 70% represents top academic distinction, equivalent to a US **3.7 to 4.0 GPA (Grade A)**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **UK Degree Classification & US GPA Equivalency Scale**:
  - **First Class Honours (1st - 70%+)**: Equivalent to US **3.70 to 4.00 GPA** (Grade A / A+).
  - **Upper Second Class (2:1 - 60% to 69%)**: Equivalent to US **3.30 to 3.69 GPA** (Grade B+ / A-). High 2:1 requires ~3.5 GPA.
  - **Lower Second Class (2:2 - 50% to 59%)**: Equivalent to US **2.70 to 3.29 GPA** (Grade B- / B).
  - **Third Class Honours (3rd - 40% to 49%)**: Equivalent to US **2.00 to 2.69 GPA** (Grade C / C+).
  - **Fail (< 40%)**: Equivalent to US **< 2.00 GPA** (Grade D / F).
- **Postgraduate Master's Entry Threshold**: Most UK Russell Group universities mandate a **2:1 undergraduate degree requirement**, translating to a minimum **3.30 to 3.50 US GPA**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Directly Translating Percentages**: Assuming a UK 70% is a US 'C' grade is a major error; UK university marks above 70% are distinction-level marks.
- **Ignoring University-Specific Conversion Tables**: Highly competitive UK universities (e.g. Oxford or Cambridge) may require a 3.75+ GPA for courses specifying a 2:1 minimum requirement.
- **Confusing Weighted vs Unweighted GPA**: UK ENIC uses unweighted cumulative GPA when evaluating international transcripts for UK university equivalence.
