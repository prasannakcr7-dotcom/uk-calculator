In payroll administration, accounting, and legal billing, recording working time in standard clock time (hours and minutes) can cause significant calculation errors if multiplied directly by hourly pay rates.

Converting clock time into **industrial minutes (decimal time)** converts the 60-minute hour into a 100-unit base, allowing standard decimal multiplication on payroll systems.

## ⚙️ Industrial Decimal Conversion Math & Lookup Benchmarks

### 1. Mathematical Conversion Formula
To convert clock minutes into industrial decimal hours:
- **Formula**: `Decimal Hours = Hours + (Clock Minutes / 60)`
- **Industrial Minutes**: The fractional decimal portion multiplied by 100 is referred to as "industrial minutes."

### 2. Common Clock Minutes Conversion Table
- **6 Minutes**: 6 / 60 = **0.10 Decimal Hours** (10 Industrial Minutes)
- **12 Minutes**: 12 / 60 = **0.20 Decimal Hours** (20 Industrial Minutes)
- **15 Minutes**: 15 / 60 = **0.25 Decimal Hours** (25 Industrial Minutes)
- **30 Minutes**: 30 / 60 = **0.50 Decimal Hours** (50 Industrial Minutes)
- **45 Minutes**: 45 / 60 = **0.75 Decimal Hours** (75 Industrial Minutes)
- **48 Minutes**: 48 / 60 = **0.80 Decimal Hours** (80 Industrial Minutes)

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Payroll Warnings

1. **The 30-Minute Decimal Trap**: Entering 7 hours and 30 minutes into payroll software as `7.30` instead of `7.50` results in multiplying by 7.30 hours, underpaying the worker by 12 minutes (0.20 hours) per shift!
2. **Rounding Errors on Non-Standard Minutes**: Clock minutes that do not divide cleanly by 60 (e.g. 7 minutes = 0.11666... hours) should be rounded to 2 or 4 decimal places according to your payroll software specification to avoid penny discrepancies.
3. **Confusing Industrial Time with 24-Hour Military Time**: Military time (e.g. 14:30) represents time of day, whereas industrial time (e.g. 2.50 hours) represents a duration of elapsed time.
