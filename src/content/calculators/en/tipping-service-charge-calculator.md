Tipping customs and service charge rules in the UK differ from countries like the United States. In the UK, tipping is genuinely voluntary, and workers are paid at least the statutory National Minimum Wage (£12.21/hr in 2026/27) regardless of tips received.

Under the landmark **Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023**, UK employers are legally prohibited from withholding or deducting any portion of tips, gratuities, or service charges from hospitality staff. 100% of tips paid by customers (whether by card or cash) must be passed directly to workers without administrative deductions.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Standard UK Tipping Benchmarks**:
  - **Outside London / Casual Dining**: **10%** is standard for good table service.
  - **London & Fine Dining**: A discretionary service charge of **12.5%** is automatically added to restaurant bills.
  - **Pubs & Bars**: Tipping for drinks at a pub bar is not expected, though offering "and one for yourself" is a traditional gesture.
- **Discretionary Service Charge Removal**: Any service charge printed as "optional" or "discretionary" on your bill can legally be removed or adjusted upon request before paying.
- **Allocation of Tips Act 2023 Compliance**: Employers must distribute 100% of card and cash tips to workers by the end of the month following the customer's payment, governed by a written workplace tipping policy.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Double Tipping**: If your bill already includes a 12.5% "optional service charge", adding an extra cash tip results in tipping twice for the same meal!
- **Assuming Card Tips Are Kept by Management**: Prior to 2023, some restaurant owners kept card tips; under current UK law, card tips must legally be passed 100% to staff.
- **Paying Discretionary Charges for Poor Service**: You have a legal right under consumer law to ask the restaurant manager to remove the service charge if table service was unacceptably poor.
