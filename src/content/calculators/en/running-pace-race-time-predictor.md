Whether training for a Saturday morning **5k Parkrun**, preparing for a **10k race**, targeting a sub-2 hour **Half Marathon (Great North Run)**, or pacing the **London Marathon (42.2 km / 26.2 miles)**, calculating your required **running pace per kilometre** or **per mile** is essential for effective race execution and training.

In sports science, predicting equivalent finish times across longer or shorter race distances relies on **Riegel's Formula**, an empirical endurance equation (`T2 = T1 × (D2 / D1)^1.06`) developed by engineer Peter Riegel to model aerobic fatigue over increasing distance.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Pace Calculation Formulas**:
  - `Pace Per KM = Total Race Seconds / Total Kilometres`.
  - `Pace Per Mile = Total Race Seconds / Total Miles`.
  - `Average Speed (km/h) = Distance in KM / Total Hours`.
  - `Average Speed (mph) = Distance in Miles / Total Hours`.
- **Riegel's Race Time Prediction Formula**:
  `T2 = T1 × (D2 / D1)^1.06`
  Where `T1` is known time, `D1` is known distance, `D2` is target race distance, and `T2` is predicted finish time.
- **Popular UK Race Distances**:
  - **5k Parkrun**: 5.0 km (3.107 miles).
  - **10k Road Race**: 10.0 km (6.214 miles).
  - **Half Marathon**: 21.0975 km (13.109 miles).
  - **Full Marathon**: 42.195 km (26.219 miles).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Starting Too Fast (Positive Splitting)**: Running the first 2 km significantly faster than your target pace consumes glycolytic energy stores prematurely, leading to severe slowdown ("hitting the wall") later in the race.
- **Assuming Riegel's Formula Holds Without Distance Training**: Riegel's formula assumes an athlete has completed adequate long-distance endurance training to support the longer target distance.
- **Confusing Min/KM with Min/Mile**: Running a 5:00 min/mile pace is elite sprinting speed (12 mph), whereas a 5:00 min/km pace is a moderate recreational run (7.5 mph).
