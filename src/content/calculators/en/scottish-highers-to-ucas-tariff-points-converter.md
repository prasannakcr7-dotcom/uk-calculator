In Scotland, secondary school students taking **SQA qualifications** (Scottish Qualifications Authority) earn **Highers** (usually taken in S5) and **Advanced Highers** (taken in S6) for Scottish and UK university entry.

While Scottish universities (such as Edinburgh, Glasgow, St Andrews, and Aberdeen) often state entry offers as letter grades across 4 or 5 Highers (for example, **AAAB**), universities in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland frequently express entry requirements as a total **UCAS Tariff score**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Official SQA Higher UCAS Tariff Points**:
  - **Grade A**: **33 UCAS Points**
  - **Grade B**: **27 UCAS Points**
  - **Grade C**: **21 UCAS Points**
  - **Grade D**: **15 UCAS Points**
- **Official SQA Advanced Higher UCAS Tariff Points**:
  - **Grade A\***: **56 UCAS Points** (same as A-Level A\*)
  - **Grade A**: **48 UCAS Points** (same as A-Level A)
  - **Grade B**: **40 UCAS Points** (same as A-Level B)
  - **Grade C**: **32 UCAS Points** (same as A-Level C)
  - **Grade D**: **24 UCAS Points** (same as A-Level D)
- **Combining Highers and Advanced Highers**: Students progressing from S5 to S6 can add UCAS points earned in Advanced Highers to their existing S5 Highers, provided the Advanced Higher is in a different or advanced subject module.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing Higher Points with Advanced Higher Points**: An A at SQA Higher is worth 33 points, whereas an A at Advanced Higher is worth 48 points.
- **Overlapping Same Subject Grades**: UCAS rules forbid counting a Higher and an Advanced Higher in the exact same subject if the university specifies non-duplication.
- **Assuming Scottish Universities Demand Tariff Points**: Most ancient Scottish universities specify entry offers as exact Highers letter combinations (e.g. AABB or ABBB) achieved by the end of S5 or S6.
