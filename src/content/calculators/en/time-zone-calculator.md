Coordinating international business communications, remote workforce scheduling, software deployments, and virtual meetings across different global time zones requires accurate time zone conversion centered on UK local time.

The UK operates on **Greenwich Mean Time (GMT = UTC+0)** during winter months and switches to **British Summer Time (BST = UTC+1)** during daylight saving time (late March to late October). Because other nations transition to Daylight Saving Time on different dates, time differences between London and global financial centers (like New York, San Francisco, Tokyo, and Sydney) change dynamically throughout the year.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Coordinated Universal Time (UTC / GMT)**: The world standard time reference against which all global time zone offsets are measured.
- **UK Seasonal Adjustments**:
  - **GMT (UTC+0)**: Last Sunday in October to last Sunday in March.
  - **BST (UTC+1)**: Last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October.
- **Major Global Offsets Relative to London (Winter GMT)**:
  - **New York (US Eastern)**: UTC-5 (5 hours behind London).
  - **San Francisco (US Pacific)**: UTC-8 (8 hours behind London).
  - **Dubai (GST)**: UTC+4 (4 hours ahead of London).
  - **Singapore / Hong Kong**: UTC+8 (8 hours ahead of London).
  - **Tokyo (JST)**: UTC+9 (9 hours ahead of London).
  - **Sydney (AEST)**: UTC+10 (10 hours ahead of London in UK winter).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Ignoring Asynchronous Daylight Saving Transitions**: The US switches to Daylight Saving Time 2 to 3 weeks before the UK in March; during this transition window, New York is only 4 hours behind London instead of 5!
- **Scheduling Meetings Across the International Date Line**: Scheduling a call for 16:00 London time on Friday results in 02:00 AM **Saturday morning** local time in Sydney (+1 day shift).
- **Confusing GMT with BST**: Assuming the UK is always on GMT leads to 1-hour errors during the 7 months of the year when the UK is on BST (UTC+1).

    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between GMT, BST, and UTC?</button>

      <p>UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the global scientific time standard. GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) is the UK standard winter time zone (identical to UTC+0). BST (British Summer Time) is the UK daylight saving time zone (UTC+1) used between late March and late October.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Why do US and UK time differences change temporarily in March and October?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The US switches to Daylight Saving Time on the second Sunday in March, whereas the UK switches to BST on the last Sunday in March. During those intervening weeks, the time difference between London and New York narrows to 4 hours instead of the usual 5 hours.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the best time window for a joint meeting between London, New York, and San Francisco?</button>

      <p>The optimal window for a joint UK-US cross-coastal call is between 16:00 and 17:30 London time. This corresponds to 11:00 to 12:30 in New York (Eastern) and 08:00 to 09:30 in San Francisco (Pacific).</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does Japan or Singapore use Daylight Saving Time?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Countries such as Japan (JST = UTC+9), Singapore (SGT = UTC+8), and UAE (GST = UTC+4) do not observe Daylight Saving Time. Their time zone offsets relative to UTC remain fixed throughout the entire year.</p>
