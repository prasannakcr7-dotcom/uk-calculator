An **Early Repayment Charge (ERC)** is a penalty fee charged by UK mortgage lenders if you overpay more than your permitted annual allowance or exit a fixed-rate or discounted mortgage contract early.

Understanding your **10% annual penalty-free overpayment allowance** and calculating your exact **ERC percentage penalty** prevents costly financial surprises when making lump-sum overpayments or remortgaging before your fixed rate expires.

## ⚙️ Statutory ERC Rules & Allowance Calculations for 2026/27

### 1. The 10% Annual Penalty-Free Overpayment Allowance
Most UK mortgage lenders permit borrowers to overpay up to **10% of their outstanding mortgage balance** (or original loan balance) each year without incurring any ERC fee:
- *Example*: On an outstanding mortgage balance of **£200,000**, your 10% penalty-free allowance is **£20,000 per year**.
- If you overpay **£50,000** as a lump sum, the first **£20,000 is 100% penalty-free**, leaving **£30,000 subject to the ERC penalty rate**.

### 2. Stepped Early Repayment Charge Scales
ERC fees on fixed-rate mortgages usually decrease step-by-step each year during the fixed rate term:
- **5-Year Fixed Deal Typical ERC Scale**:
  - Year 1: **5% penalty**
  - Year 2: **4% penalty**
  - Year 3: **3% penalty**
  - Year 4: **2% penalty**
  - Year 5: **1% penalty**
  - After Fixed Expiry (SVR): **0% (Zero ERC penalty)**

### 3. Statutory Porting Exceptions & SVR
- **Porting Your Mortgage**: If you move home during a fixed-rate term, most UK lenders allow you to **port (transfer)** your existing mortgage rate and balance to the new property without paying an ERC penalty.
- **Standard Variable Rate (SVR)**: Mortgages on an SVR or tracker product with no tie-in period carry zero ERC penalties.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & ERC Warnings

1. **Timing Lump Sums Across Calendar vs Overpayment Years**: Overpayment allowances reset either every 12 months from your mortgage start date or every calendar year (1st January). Timing large overpayments to span across two allowance periods (e.g. £20k on Dec 31 and £20k on Jan 2) avoids thousands in ERC fees.
2. **Remortgaging a Few Weeks Too Early**: Switching your mortgage to a new lender just 2 weeks before your fixed-rate deal officially expires triggers a full ERC penalty (e.g. 1% = £2,000). Always align completion dates with your fixed rate expiry date.
3. **Monthly Overpayment Accrual Limits**: Monthly regular overpayments count toward your 10% annual allowance. Ensure you monitor cumulative regular overpayments before making an additional lump-sum payment.

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>If you sell your home during a fixed-rate term and move into rented accommodation or do not purchase a new property within your lender's porting window, you must pay the full Early Repayment Charge (ERC) fee directly from your property sale proceeds.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Are monthly regular overpayments subject to ERC fees? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No, monthly regular overpayments are not charged an ERC fee provided your total cumulative overpayments (including regular monthly overpayments and lump sums) remain within your 10% annual penalty-free allowance limit specified by your lender.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can an ERC fee be added to a new mortgage loan? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>If you switch lenders early, the ERC penalty is charged by your old lender and deducted from your property equity or added to your new mortgage borrowing balance.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Why do UK banks charge Early Repayment Charges? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Lenders hedge money on wholesale money markets to fund fixed-rate mortgages. If a borrower exits early, the lender incurs financial hedging losses, which the ERC fee recovers.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
