Night work in the UK is legally defined as work performed between **23:00 and 06:00** (or an agreed 7-hour block including midnight to 05:00).

Understanding contractual night shift pay premiums—and statutory health and safety limits—ensures night workers receive correct enhanced remuneration.

## ⚙️ UK Night Work Regulations & Premium Rates for 2026/27

### 1. Contractual Night Shift Allowance Premiums
While UK employment law does not establish an automatic statutory right to higher pay rates for night work, employers commonly pay a contractual **night premium** to compensate for unsocial hours:
- **Common Night Premiums**: Range between **15% and 33%** above daytime base rates (25% is standard across healthcare, logistics, and manufacturing).

### 2. Working Time Directive (WTD) Night Limits
Under **Regulation 6 of the Working Time Regulations 1998**:
- **8-Hour Average Limit**: Night workers must not work more than an average of **8 hours in a 24-hour period** (calculated over a 17-week reference period).
- **Heavy / Hazardous Night Work**: If a night job involves special hazards or heavy physical/mental strain, the 8-hour limit is an **absolute daily cap**, not an average.

### 3. Statutory Free Health Assessments
Employers must offer all night workers a **free health assessment** before they start night work and at regular annual intervals thereafter.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Night Work Warnings

1. **Young Workers Under 18 Restriction**: Young workers under 18 years of age are strictly prohibited from working between 22:00 and 06:00 (or 23:00 and 07:00) except in exceptional circumstances.
2. **Ignoring National Minimum Wage Compliance**: If an employer does not offer a night premium, the base hourly rate for all night hours worked must still comply fully with statutory National Minimum Wage (£12.21/hr for 21+).
3. **Failing to Transfer Workers Unfit for Night Duty**: If a medical health assessment indicates that a night worker is suffering from health issues related to night shifts, the employer must transfer them to suitable daytime work wherever possible.
