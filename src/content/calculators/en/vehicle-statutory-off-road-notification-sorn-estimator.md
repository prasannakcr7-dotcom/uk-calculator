If you plan to take your car, motorcycle, motorhome, or van off UK public roads for an extended period—for example, during winter storage, while undertaking repairs, or while traveling abroad—declaring a **Statutory Off Road Notification (SORN)** with the DVLA is legally mandatory to avoid tax and insurance penalties.

Declaring SORN automatically cancels your vehicle's road tax (Vehicle Excise Duty - VED) and triggers an **automatic DVLA refund check** for any full remaining unexpired calendar months of prepaid road tax.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **DVLA Road Tax Refund Rule**:
  - Refunds are calculated for **full remaining complete calendar months**.
  - DVLA issues a refund cheque automatically to the registered keeper listed on the V5C logbook within 4 to 6 weeks of SORN declaration.
- **Strict Storage Location Rules**:
  - **LEGAL SORN Storage**: Private driveway, private garage, private underground space, or private land.
  - **ILLEGAL SORN Storage**: Any public highway, public council road, or public resident parking bay. Leaving a SORN vehicle on a public road triggers fixed penalty fines up to **£1,000**, plus immediate vehicle wheel clamping or impounding.
- **Continuous Insurance Enforcement (CIE)**: Under UK CIE laws, every registered vehicle MUST be continuously insured UNLESS a valid SORN is registered with the DVLA.
- **Exceptions for Driving a SORN Vehicle**: You may legally drive a SORN vehicle on public roads ONLY when driving directly to or from a pre-booked MOT test appointment.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Parking a SORN Vehicle on a Public Road**: Parking a SORN vehicle on a public street or residential council bay—even outside your house—is a criminal offence under VERA 1994.
- **Forgetting to SORN an Uninsured Car Kept in a Garage**: Leaving an uninsured car in your garage without registering a SORN triggers an automatic £100 DVLA Continuous Insurance Enforcement (CIE) fine.
- **Expecting Partial Month Road Tax Refunds**: DVLA does not refund partial months; declaring SORN on the 2nd day of a month means that entire month's tax is forfeited.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How do I declare SORN with the DVLA?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>You can declare SORN online for free at GOV.UK using the 11-digit reference number from your V5C logbook (effective immediately) or the 16-digit reference number from your V11 tax reminder letter (effective from the 1st of the following month).</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does a SORN declaration expire after 12 months?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Under modern DVLA regulations, a SORN declaration remains active indefinitely until you re-tax the vehicle, sell it, scrap it at an Authorised Treatment Facility, or export it abroad.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can I drive a SORN car to an MOT test?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes, under strict conditions. You are legally allowed to drive a SORN vehicle on a public road ONLY to and from a pre-booked MOT test appointment, provided you have valid third-party insurance in place for the journey.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How do I cancel SORN when I want to use my car again?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>To cancel SORN, simply pay your vehicle road tax (VED) online via GOV.UK and ensure you have valid MOT and car insurance in place. Taxing the vehicle automatically cancels the SORN declaration instantly.</p>
    </div>
