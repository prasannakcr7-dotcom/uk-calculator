Child maintenance is a financial arrangement between separated parents to help cover living costs for a child under 16 (or under 20 if in full-time non-advanced education).

The **Child Maintenance Service (CMS)** uses a statutory formula based on the paying parent's gross weekly income, the number of qualifying children, and the number of overnight stays spent with the paying parent.

## ⚙️ CMS Statutory Rates & Shared Care Discount Rules

### 1. CMS Percentage Rate Bands (Gross Weekly Income)
- **Basic Rate (£200 to £800/wk)**:
  - 1 Child: **12%** of gross weekly income.
  - 2 Children: **16%** of gross weekly income.
  - 3+ Children: **19%** of gross weekly income.
- **Basic Plus Rate (£800 to £3,000/wk)**:
  - Additional income between £800 and £3,000 is taxed at reduced percentages (9% for 1 child, 12% for 2 children, 15% for 3+ children).
- **Reduced Rate (£100 to £200/wk)**: Fixed base plus reduced percentage.
- **Flat Rate (£7 to £100/wk)**: Fixed flat payment of **£7.00 per week**.

### 2. Shared Care Overnight Discounts
If the child stays overnight with the paying parent on average at least 1 night a week (52 nights a year), maintenance payments are discounted:
- **52 to 103 nights/yr**: **1/7th deduction** (14.3% discount).
- **104 to 155 nights/yr**: **2/7ths deduction** (28.6% discount).
- **156 to 174 nights/yr**: **3/7ths deduction** (42.9% discount).
- **175+ nights/yr**: **50% deduction plus extra £7/week reduction per child**.

## 📊 Practical Child Maintenance Worked Examples

Below are two worked calculation examples demonstrating statutory CMS calculations:

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 1: Paying parent earning £600 gross/week with 1 child (no overnight stays)</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Gross weekly income: **£600.00**</li>
    <li>Qualifying children: **1 Child**</li>
    <li>CMS Basic Rate: **12%**</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Calculation: £600.00 × 12% = £72.00 per week. Shared care discount = £0.00.</p>
  <div class="net-highlight">Weekly Child Maintenance Payable: **£72.00 / week** (£312.00 per month)</div>
</div>

<div class="example-box">
  <strong>Example 2: Paying parent earning £600 gross/week with 1 child (60 overnight stays/yr)</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Base weekly calculation: **£72.00 / week**</li>
    <li>Overnight stays: **60 nights / year** (52 to 103 band)</li>
    <li>Shared care discount: **1/7th deduction** (£72.00 ÷ 7 = £10.29 reduction)</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Calculation: £72.00 - £10.29 = £61.71 per week.</p>
  <div class="net-highlight">Weekly Child Maintenance Payable: **£61.71 / week** (£267.41 per month)</div>
</div>

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Legal Warnings

1. **Calculating on Net Salary Instead of Gross Income**: The CMS scheme introduced in 2012 calculates maintenance using **gross weekly income** (before Income Tax and NI) as reported to HMRC via PAYE, not net take-home pay.
2. **Ignoring Pension Contributions**: Contributions to a pension scheme are deducted from gross income before the CMS percentage is applied. Increasing workplace pension contributions reduces your statutory child maintenance liability while increasing your retirement fund.
3. **Failing to Document Shared Care Overnight Stays**: If parents disagree on overnight care frequencies, the CMS may ask for utility bills, school calendars, or medical records to verify claims. Keeping a clear shared care log is essential.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What happens if the paying parent refuses to pay child maintenance? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The Child Maintenance Service (CMS) has legal enforcement powers to collect unpaid maintenance directly from wages via a Deduction from Earnings Order, take money from bank accounts, or take legal action through the courts.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How long is a parent legally obligated to pay child maintenance? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Child maintenance must be paid until the child reaches age 16, or age 20 if they remain in full-time non-advanced education (such as A-levels or college courses). Payments stop if the child enters higher university education or full-time employment.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does child maintenance affect Universal Credit benefit payments? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Child maintenance received by a caring parent is completely ignored as income when calculating Universal Credit payments. You keep 100% of your Universal Credit alongside child maintenance.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Is child maintenance taxable income for the receiving parent? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. Child maintenance payments are completely tax-free for the receiving parent. You do not need to report child maintenance income on a Self Assessment tax return.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
