Pay As You Earn (PAYE) is the UK system used by employers to deduct Income Tax, Class 1 National Insurance Contributions (NICs), student loan repayments, and workplace pension contributions directly from employee wages before paying net take-home salary.

Understanding your monthly PAYE payslip deductions ensures full visibility over your household spendable cash.

## ⚙️ Statutory PAYE Payroll Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Tax-Free Personal Allowance (Tax Code 1257L)
- **Standard Tax-Free Threshold**: **£12,570 per year** (£1,047.50/month; £241.73/week).
- **Tax Code 1257L**: Indicates an employee receives the full statutory £12,570 tax-free Personal Allowance.

### 2. Income Tax Band Deductions (England, Wales, NI)
- **Basic Rate Band (£12,571 to £50,270)**: **20% Income Tax**.
- **Higher Rate Band (£50,271 to £125,140)**: **40% Income Tax**.
- **Additional Rate Band (Above £125,140)**: **45% Income Tax**.

### 3. Employee Class 1 National Insurance (NI) Rates
- **Earnings up to £12,570**: **0% NI**.
- **Earnings between £12,571 and £50,270**: **8% NI**.
- **Earnings above £50,270**: **2% NI**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & PAYE Warnings

1. **Emergency Tax Codes (W1/M1 or 0T)**: If you start a new job without handing your P45 to HR, you may be assigned an emergency tax code. Emergency codes apply tax on a non-cumulative basis, causing temporary over-taxation until HMRC updates your PAYE code.
2. **K Tax Codes (Benefits in Kind Surcharge)**: A 'K' prefix tax code (e.g. K475) indicates that taxable benefits in kind (such as a company car or private medical insurance) exceed your Personal Allowance, resulting in higher monthly PAYE deductions.
3. **Cumulative vs Non-Cumulative PAYE Math**: PAYE is cumulative by default. If your salary fluctuates month-to-month, PAYE automatically recalculates your tax position across all previous months in the tax year to keep deductions accurate.
