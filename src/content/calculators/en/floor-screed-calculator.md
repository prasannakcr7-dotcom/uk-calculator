Laying a smooth **semi-dry sand and cement floor screed** or **flowing liquid screed** over subfloor concrete or underfloor heating (UFH) pipes is standard practice in UK building extensions and new builds.

Calculating total **screed volume in cubic metres (m³)** alongside raw **25kg cement bags** and **sharp sand bulk bags (850kg)** ensures adequate material delivery while adhering to **BS 8204 minimum depth standards**.

## ⚙️ Statutory Screed Depths & Mix Proportions for 2026/27

### 1. Minimum Statutory Screed Depths (BS 8204)
Minimum screed thickness depends on how the screed is bonded to the underlying base:
- **Bonded Screed (Direct to Concrete Base)**: **40mm minimum thickness** (Slurry bonding bridge required).
- **Unbonded Screed (Laid Over DPM Membrane)**: **50mm minimum thickness**.
- **Floating Screed (Over Insulation & Underfloor Heating)**:
  - Traditional Sand & Cement: **65mm minimum thickness** for domestic; **75mm** for commercial.
  - Flowing Liquid Anhydrite Screed: **45mm minimum thickness** (or 30mm coverage over 16mm UFH pipes).

### 2. Standard 1:4 Screed Mix Proportions (By Volume)
- **1 Part Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) : 4 Parts Clean Sharp Sand**.
- **Density**: Dry sand and cement screed weighs approximately **2,000 kg per cubic metre (2.0 tonnes/m³)**.
- *Example*: Screeding a **30.0 sq m room at 65mm depth (0.065m)**:
  - `Volume = 30.0m × 0.065m = 1.95 m³`.
  - `Total Mass = 1.95 m³ × 2,000 kg/m³ = 3,900 kg`.
  - `Cement (1/5th weight) = 780 kg ÷ 25kg = 31 Bags of Cement`.
  - `Sharp Sand (4/5ths weight) = 3,120 kg ÷ 850kg = 3.7 Bulk Bags of Sharp Sand`.

### 3. Drying Time & Moisture Content Rules
- **Traditional Sand & Cement**: Dries at **1mm per day** for the first 40mm depth under ideal drying conditions (20°C / 50% humidity). Depths beyond 40mm dry at 0.5mm per day. (A 65mm screed requires **~90 days drying time** before laying impervious vinyl or wood flooring).
- **Accelerated Screeds**: Chemical additives reduce drying times to 7–14 days.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Floor Screeding Warnings

1. **Using Building Sand Instead of Sharp Sand**: Never use soft building sand for floor screeds. Soft sand causes cracking, crumbling, and poor compressive strength. Always specify washed **0-4mm Sharp Sand (Concreting Sand)**.
2. **Laying Screed Too Thin Over UFH Insulation**: Laying a floating sand & cement screed thinner than 65mm over insulation insulation boards causes the screed to flex and crack under foot traffic.
3. **Failing to Install Perimeter Expansion Strips**: Edge insulation foam strips (8mm–10mm) MUST line all wall perimeters when laying screeds over underfloor heating to allow thermal expansion without pushing against walls.

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Light foot traffic is permitted after **24 to 48 hours**. Heavy construction traffic and trades should be avoided for at least 7 days.</p>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">When can underfloor heating be turned on after screeding? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Underfloor heating must NOT be turned on for at least **21 days** after laying traditional sand and cement screed. Commissioning must begin slowly at 15°C water flow temperature, increasing by 5°C per day.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is polypropylene fibre reinforcement in screeds? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Adding polypropylene PP fibres (approx 900g per m³) into the screed mix reduces plastic shrinkage cracking and improves impact resistance, replacing traditional steel D49 mesh.</p>
