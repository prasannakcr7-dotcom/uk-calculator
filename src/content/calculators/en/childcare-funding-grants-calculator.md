Childcare funding in the UK provides substantial government subsidies to lower early years nursery, preschool, and childminder expenses for working families.

With the rollout of the **expanded 30 free hours scheme**, eligible working parents of children aged 9 months up to school age can claim funded childcare hours alongside **Tax-Free Childcare** savings.

## ⚙️ Statutory Schemes & Eligibility Criteria for 2026/27

### 1. 30 Free Hours Scheme (Aged 9 Months to 4 Years)
- **Entitlement**: Up to **30 hours per week** of funded early education during term time (calculated across 38 weeks per year = **1,140 hours/year**).
- **Earnings Requirement**: Both parents (or a single parent) must earn at least the equivalent of **16 hours per week at National Minimum Wage** (approx. £10,100/year each).
- **Maximum Income Cap**: Neither parent can have an adjusted net income exceeding **£100,000 per year**.

### 2. Tax-Free Childcare (20% Government Top-Up)
- **Subsidy**: For every £8 you pay into your online Tax-Free Childcare account, the government pays an extra £2.
- **Maximum Benefit**: Up to **£2,000 per child per year** (or £4,000 for a disabled child).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Eligibility Traps

1. **The Hard £100,000 Income Cliff Edge**: If either parent earns **£100,001 or more** in adjusted net income, you lose **100% eligibility** for both 30 free hours and Tax-Free Childcare. Salary sacrifice into pensions can lower income below £100k to preserve thousands in subsidies.
2. **Forgetting 3-Month Code Re-Confirmations**: Parents must re-confirm their eligibility online via their government gateway account every **3 months** to keep their funded hours code active.
3. **Nursery Meals and Consumable Extras**: Free childcare hours cover early education tuition, but nurseries are permitted to charge optional extra fees for meals, snacks, nappies, and outings.

      <p>Yes. Many nurseries allow parents to 'stretch' their total 1,140 annual free hours across 50 or 52 weeks of the year, which equates to roughly 22 free hours per week year-round.</p>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between Tax-Free Childcare and Universal Credit childcare help? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>You cannot use Tax-Free Childcare and Universal Credit at the same time. Universal Credit offers higher help (reimbursing up to 85% of costs up to £1,014/mo for 1 child), whereas Tax-Free Childcare gives a 20% bonus up to £2,000/yr.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">When do free childcare hours start after my child turns 9 months or 3 years old? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Free hours start from the term after your child reaches the qualifying age (terms begin September 1st, January 1st, and April 1st). You must apply for your code before the start of the term.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does self-employment qualify for 30 free childcare hours? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Self-employed parents qualify if their average earnings meet the minimum requirement (equivalent to 16 hours/wk at minimum wage). Newly self-employed parents get a 12-month start-up exemption period.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
