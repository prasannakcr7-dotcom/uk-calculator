Deciding between a **petrol car** and a **diesel car** in the UK requires balancing diesel's superior long-distance fuel economy (**50 to 60+ MPG**) against higher **diesel pump prices per litre** and upfront vehicle purchase premiums.

While diesel cars offer **15% to 25% better fuel efficiency** on long motorway journeys, drivers who cover lower annual mileage (**under 10,000 miles/year**) or drive short urban city trips often spend more money on diesel car ownership due to higher purchase prices and **Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF)** repair costs.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Fuel Calculations & Conversion Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Imperial Gallon Fuel Cost Formulas
In the UK, fuel efficiency is measured in Miles Per Gallon (MPG), but fuel is sold at forecourts in Litres.
- **UK Imperial Gallon Conversion**: `1 UK Imperial Gallon = 4.54609 Litres`.
- **Fuel Cost per Mile (p/mi)**: `Cost per Mile (£) = [ Price per Litre (£) × 4.54609 ] ÷ Vehicle MPG`.
- **Annual Fuel Bill (£)**: `Annual Fuel Cost = Annual Mileage × Cost per Mile`.
- *Example*: Comparing a **Petrol car (40 MPG @ £1.45/L)** against a **Diesel car (55 MPG @ £1.52/L)** over **12,000 miles/year**:
  - `Petrol Cost per Mile = (£1.45 × 4.54609) ÷ 40 = 16.48 pence / mile`.
  - `Diesel Cost per Mile = (£1.52 × 4.54609) ÷ 55 = 12.56 pence / mile`.
  - `Annual Petrol Bill = 12,000 × 16.48p = £1,977.56 / year`.
  - `Annual Diesel Bill = 12,000 × 12.56p = £1,507.20 / year`.
  - `Annual Diesel Savings = £1,977.56 – £1,507.20 = £470.36 per year`.

### 2. Diesel Purchase Price Premium Breakeven
- Diesel cars typically cost **£1,000 to £2,500 more** to buy than equivalent petrol models.
- **Breakeven Payback Years**: `Payback Years = Diesel Purchase Premium (£) ÷ Annual Fuel Savings (£)`.
- *Example*: Paying a **£1,500 diesel purchase premium** with **£470.36 annual fuel savings**:
  - `Payback Period = £1,500 ÷ £470.36 = 3.2 Years`.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Fuel Economy Warnings

1. **The Short-Journey DPF Clogging Trap**: Diesel vehicles feature a **Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF)** that traps soot. DPF filters require long motorway journeys at 2,000+ RPM to reach 600°C and burn off soot ('passive regeneration'). Driving short urban school runs causes DPF filters to clog, requiring **£1,000 to £2,000 replacement costs**.
2. **Confusing UK Imperial Gallons with US Gallons**: US fuel calculators use US Gallons (3.785 Litres), which understates MPG figures by 20%. Always ensure you use UK **Imperial Gallons (4.54609 Litres)**.
3. **Clean Air Zone (CAZ) & ULEZ Charges**: Older Euro 5 diesel cars (pre-2015) do NOT meet London ULEZ or Birmingham/Bristol CAZ standards, incurring a **£12.50 daily charge** to enter city centers. Euro 6 petrol cars (post-2006) are exempt.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Why is diesel fuel more expensive than petrol in the UK? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Diesel pump prices are higher in the UK due to higher global refining costs for middle-distillate fuels, increased winter heating oil demand across Europe, and import supply chain dynamics.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What annual mileage is needed to justify buying a diesel car? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>In the UK, drivers covering at least **12,000 to 15,000+ miles per year** (primarily on dual carriageways and motorways) generate enough fuel savings to justify a diesel vehicle.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is AdBlue / DEF fluid in modern diesel cars? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>AdBlue (Diesel Exhaust Fluid) is a urea-based solution injected into Euro 6 diesel exhausts to convert toxic Nitrogen Oxide (NOx) gases into harmless nitrogen and water vapour. Refilling AdBlue costs ~£15 every 5,000 to 10,000 miles.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the difference between WLTP and real-world MPG? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>WLTP (Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicles Test Procedure) is the official laboratory fuel economy test. Real-world MPG is typically **10% to 15% lower** due to traffic, weather, air conditioning, and payload.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
