Planning a wedding in the UK requires careful financial management. The average cost of a UK wedding currently stands between **£18,000 and £24,000**, with venue hire and guest catering accounting for nearly half of total expenditure.

To avoid going into debt or overspending, professional UK wedding planners recommend applying the **45-15-10 Allocation Rule**, dividing your overall budget into proportional categories and reserving a mandatory **10% contingency buffer** for last-minute expenses.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Recommended UK Category Allocation Ratios**:
  - **Venue Hire, Food & Drinks (45%)**: The largest single expense. Includes venue room hire, wedding breakfast, welcome drinks, table wine, and evening buffet.
  - **Music, Flowers & Decor (15%)**: DJ, live band, floral arrangements, table centerpieces, lighting, and room styling.
  - **Photography & Videography (10%)**: Professional photographer and videographer coverage.
  - **Attire, Wedding Rings & Beauty (10%)**: Wedding dress, suit hire, rings, hair, and makeup.
  - **Honeymoon (10%)**: Flights, accommodation, and spending money.
  - **Contingency Fund (10%)**: Essential safety net for hidden costs (corkage fees, VAT additions, supplier travel fees).
- **Statutory Ceremony Costs**: Registrar attendance fees and legal notice of marriage fees (approx. £40 per person notice fee + £500–£700 local council registrar fee for approved venues).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Forgetting Service VAT and Corkage Fees**: Venue quotes often exclude 20% VAT or charge £15–£25 per bottle "corkage fees" on self-supplied wine.
- **Inviting Too Many Day Guests**: Catering is a linear per-head cost; adding 20 extra day guests at £80/head inflates your budget by £1,600 immediately.
- **Skipping the 10% Contingency Buffer**: Over-allocating 100% of your budget before booking suppliers leaves zero margin for price increases or emergency alterations.
