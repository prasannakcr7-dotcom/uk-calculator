If you operate as a **sole trader** or partner in a business partnership in the UK, you pay **National Insurance Contributions (NICs)** on your net self-employed profits via your annual **Self Assessment Tax Return**.

Under recent HMRC tax reforms, mandatory Class 2 National Insurance was abolished for self-employed individuals earning above the Small Profits Threshold. Self-employed workers now pay **Class 4 NICs** on profits above the Lower Profits Limit, while receiving **free Class 2 National Insurance credits** to protect their UK State Pension entitlement.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Class 2 National Insurance Rules (2026/27)**:
  - **Profits Above Small Profits Threshold (£6,725)**: Mandatory Class 2 payments abolished (**£0.00 payable**). You automatically receive a Class 2 credit treating National Insurance as paid for State Pension purposes.
  - **Profits Below £6,725**: You can pay **Voluntary Class 2 NICs** (£3.45 per week / £179.40 per year) to fill gaps in your State Pension record.
- **Class 4 National Insurance Thresholds & Rates (2026/27)**:
  - **Lower Profits Limit (LPL)**: **£12,570 per year** (0% NIC below this threshold).
  - **Upper Profits Limit (UPL)**: **£50,270 per year**.
  - **Main Class 4 Rate**: **6%** on net profits between £12,570 and £50,270.
  - **Upper Class 4 Rate**: **2%** on net profits exceeding £50,270.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing Gross Turnover with Net Profit**: Class 4 National Insurance is calculated on **net taxable profit** (gross turnover minus allowable business expenses), not total revenue billed.
- **Neglecting Voluntary Class 2 when Profits fall below £6,725**: If your net profits fall below £6,725, failing to pay voluntary Class 2 NICs creates a missing qualifying year on your UK State Pension record.
- **Assuming Employee Class 1 Rates Apply**: Sole traders pay Class 4 NIC (6% main rate), which is lower than employee Class 1 NIC (8%), reflecting the lack of statutory employment benefits (sick pay/holiday pay).
