Under UK family law (England and Wales), dividing financial assets during a **divorce or civil partnership dissolution** is governed by **Section 25 of the Matrimonial Causes Act 1973**.

Unlike some countries with rigid mathematical formulas, UK courts use the **'Yardstick of Equality' (a 50/50 starting benchmark established in White v White)**, adjusting the final percentage split based on **housing needs, financial earnings, contributions, and the welfare of minor children**.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Divorce Rules & Section 25 Criteria for 2026/27

### 1. Section 25 Statutory Factors
When determining how matrimonial assets (property equity, bank savings, business assets, and pensions) should be divided, family courts MUST prioritize the **first consideration: the welfare of any children under 18**, followed by:
- **Income & Earning Capacity**: Current financial resources and future earning capacity of each spouse.
- **Financial Needs**: The housing and daily living needs of each spouse (and dependent children).
- **Standard of Living**: The standard of living enjoyed by the family before the breakdown of the marriage.
- **Age & Duration of Marriage**: Age of each party and duration of the marriage (including seamless cohabitation before marriage).
- **Contributions**: Contributions made by each party to the welfare of the family (including non-financial contributions as a homemaker/parent).

### 2. Matrimonial Assets vs Non-Matrimonial Assets
- **Matrimonial Assets**: Assets acquired during the marriage (the former matrimonial home, joint savings, pensions built up during marriage). These are pooled and divided.
- **Non-Matrimonial Assets**: Property or inheritances acquired before the marriage or received individually *after* separation. Non-matrimonial assets may be excluded unless needed to satisfy the primary housing needs of a spouse or children.

### 3. Pension Sharing Orders (PSO) & Pension Offsetting
- Pensions are major financial assets in UK divorces.
- **Pension Sharing Order (PSO)**: A court order allocating a specified percentage of one spouse's pension pot directly into a new pension policy for the other spouse.
- **Pension Offsetting**: One spouse retains 100% of their pension in exchange for giving up a larger share of the equity in the marital home.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Divorce Settlement Warnings

1. **Forgetting a Consent Order (Financial Clean Break)**: Decrees of divorce end the marriage, but do NOT end financial claims. Without a legally binding court **Consent Order**, an ex-spouse can make a financial claim against your future earnings, lottery wins, or inheritances **20 years later**. Always secure a formal Financial Consent Order.
2. **Ignoring Pension Capital Valuations (CEV)**: Failing to obtain official Cash Equivalent Values (CEV) for pensions leads to severe under-valuations. A £300,000 final-salary defined benefit NHS/Police pension generates far more income than a £300,000 private DC pension.
3. **Escalating Legal Court Battles Over Small Possessions**: Litigating minor disputes in Family Court burns tens of thousands in solicitor hourly rates (£250-£450/hr). Use **Family Mediation (MIAM)** to resolve financial arrangements amicably.
