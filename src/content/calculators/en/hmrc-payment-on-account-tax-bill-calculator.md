**Payments on Account** are advance prepayments toward your UK **Self Assessment tax bill** required by **HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC)**.

If your annual tax and National Insurance bill from self-employment, landlord rental income, or dividends exceeds **£1,000** (and less than 80% of your tax was collected at source via PAYE), HMRC automatically enrols you into the Payments on Account system. You must make two advance payments each year—each equal to **50% of your previous year's tax liability**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **HMRC Payment Deadlines**:
  - **31 January (Deadline 1)**: You pay the **Balancing Payment** for the tax year just ended + the **First Payment on Account (50%)** for the current tax year.
  - **31 July (Deadline 2)**: You pay the **Second Payment on Account (50%)** for the current tax year.
- **The £1,000 Exception Threshold**: You do NOT have to make Payments on Account if your total tax bill was less than £1,000, or if more than 80% of your total tax was deducted at source via PAYE.
- **Reducing Payments on Account**: If you expect your profits or income to drop in the current tax year, you can submit an online claim via GOV.UK to **reduce your Payments on Account**. However, if you reduce them lower than your actual final tax bill, HMRC charges late payment interest and penalties.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **First-Year Tax Shock**: In your first year of Self Assessment earning over £1,000 tax, your total 31 January payment is **150% of your annual tax bill** (100% balancing payment + 50% advance POA).
- **Reducing Payments on Account Incorrectly**: Artificially reducing your POAs to ease cash flow when your profits haven't actually dropped causes HMRC to backdate interest charges on the shortfall.
- **Missing the 31 July Deadline**: Many sole traders forget the 31 July deadline because it does not require filing a new tax return, triggering automatic HMRC interest charges.
