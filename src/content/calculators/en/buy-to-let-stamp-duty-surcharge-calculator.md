When purchasing a **Buy-to-Let (BTL) property** or a **second home** in England or Northern Ireland, buyers must pay higher rates of **Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT)** compared to moving into a primary main residence.

Under current HMRC rules, purchasing an additional residential property valued over £40,000 incurs a mandatory **5% Additional Property SDLT Surcharge** applied on top of standard residential SDLT tax bands across the entire purchase price.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **5% Additional Property Surcharge**: Applied across ALL price bands for buy-to-let investors, limited companies, and second home buyers.
- **2026/27 Buy-to-Let SDLT Tax Bands (England & Northern Ireland)**:
  - **£0 – £125,000**: **5%** (0% base + 5% surcharge).
  - **£125,001 – £250,000**: **7%** (2% base + 5% surcharge).
  - **£250,001 – £925,000**: **10%** (5% base + 5% surcharge).
  - **£925,001 – £1,500,000**: **15%** (10% base + 5% surcharge).
  - **Over £1,500,000**: **17%** (12% base + 5% surcharge).
- **Non-UK Resident Surcharge**: Non-UK residents buying UK residential property pay an **extra 2% surcharge** on top of the above rates (reaching up to 19% maximum SDLT).
- **36-Month Main Residence Refund Rule**: If you buy a new main home before selling your old main home, you pay the 5% surcharge upfront, but can **claim a 100% refund** of the surcharge from HMRC if you sell your previous main residence within **36 months**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Purchasing via a Limited Company to Avoid Surcharge**: Limited companies purchasing residential property pay the exact same 5% SDLT surcharge as individual buyers.
- **Forgetting Married Couple Rules**: Married couples and civil partners are treated by HMRC as a single unit; if one spouse already owns a property, buying another property in the second spouse's sole name still triggers the 5% surcharge.
- **Missing the 36-Month Refund Deadline**: Failing to complete the sale of your previous main home within 36 months forfeits your right to claim a surcharge refund from HMRC.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the Stamp Duty surcharge rate for buy-to-let properties in the UK?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>In England and Northern Ireland, buy-to-let properties and second homes are subject to a 5% SDLT surcharge on top of standard residential Stamp Duty rates. In Scotland, the Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS) rate is 6%, while in Wales the Land Transaction Tax (LTT) higher rate starts at 4%.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How can I claim a refund for the 5% Stamp Duty surcharge?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>If you paid the 5% SDLT surcharge because you bought a new main home before selling your old one, you can claim a full refund of the surcharge from HMRC if you sell your previous main residence within 36 months of buying the new property.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Do limited companies pay higher Stamp Duty on buy-to-let properties?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Limited companies pay the standard residential SDLT rates plus the 5% additional property surcharge when buying residential rental properties. For properties valued over £500,000 bought by companies for non-rental personal use, a flat 15% SDLT rate applies.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Does First-Time Buyer Stamp Duty relief apply to Buy-to-Let purchases?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>No. First-Time Buyer SDLT relief applies ONLY to individuals purchasing a property intended to be used as their main residence. If a first-time buyer purchases a property to let out to tenants, standard BTL Stamp Duty rates apply.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
