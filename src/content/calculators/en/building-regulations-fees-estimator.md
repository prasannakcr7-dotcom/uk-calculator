Submitting a **Building Control application** for a home extension, loft conversion, internal structural wall removal, or new dwelling in the UK is mandatory to ensure compliance with statutory **Building Regulations**.

Under the **Building (Local Authority Charges) Regulations 2010** and the **Building Safety Act 2022**, homeowners can choose between **Local Authority Building Control (LABC)** or a **Registered Building Control Approver (Private Approved Inspector)**, paying a fee based on the **estimated cost of building works**.

## ⚙️ Statutory Building Control Routes & Rules for 2026/27

### 1. The Three Primary Building Control Routes
- **Full Plans Application (Recommended for Major Works / Extensions)**:
  - Detailed architectural drawings, structural calculations, and thermal specs are submitted and checked *before* work starts.
  - **Fee Split**: Divided into a **Plan Charge** (payable on submission, ~35% of fee) and an **Inspection Charge** (payable after the first site inspection, ~65% of fee).
- **Building Notice Application (For Minor Alterations)**:
  - No detailed plans are checked in advance. Work proceeds directly on site with stage inspections.
  - **Fee Structure**: Paid in full upfront as a single charge (typically 10% higher than Full Plans due to higher site risk).
- **Regularisation Application (Unapproved Retrospective Work)**:
  - For unapproved past building work completed without building control consent after 1985.
  - **Statutory Surcharge**: Local authorities charge the standard fee plus a **mandatory 50% surcharge**. Regularisation fees are **VAT exempt** under HMRC rules.

### 2. Standard UK Local Authority Fee Scale (Estimated Work Values)
Fees are set locally by individual councils to recover costs:
- **Works Value £10,000 to £20,000**: Total fee ~**£450 to £550 + VAT**.
- **Works Value £20,000 to £40,000**: Total fee ~**£600 to £750 + VAT**.
- **Works Value £40,000 to £60,000**: Total fee ~**£750 to £900 + VAT**.
- **Works Value £60,000 to £100,000+**: Total fee ~**£900 to £1,200+ + VAT**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Building Control Warnings

1. **Confusing Planning Permission with Building Regulations**: Planning permission grants approval for site use and external aesthetics. **Building Regulations** cover structural integrity, fire safety, insulation (Part L), drainage (Part H), and electrical safety (Part P). You must have both.
2. **Failing to Notify Key Inspection Stages**: Building control inspectors MUST be notified at specific milestones: excavation of foundations, damp proof course (DPC) laying, oversite concrete, drainage testing, and roof timber structure before covering up.
3. **Not Securing a Completion Certificate**: Always ensure your inspector issues a formal **Building Control Completion Certificate** upon final inspection. Without this certificate, selling or remortgaging the property in future will be blocked by buyers' conveyancers.
