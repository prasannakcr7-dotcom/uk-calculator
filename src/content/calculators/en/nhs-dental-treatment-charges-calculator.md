Receiving dental treatment on the **National Health Service (NHS)** in England is divided into three fixed price **charge bands**, ensuring patients pay a fair, standardized statutory fee for clinically necessary dental care.

Whether you need a routine check-up, multiple fillings, a root canal, or new dentures, understanding **NHS Dental Bands (Band 1: £26.80, Band 2: £73.50, Band 3: £319.10)** and statutory **NHS Exemption Rules** prevents overpaying for dental care.

## ⚙️ Statutory NHS Dental Bands & Exemptions for 2026/27

### 1. The Three Statutory NHS Dental Charge Bands (England)
- **Band 1 (£26.80)**: Covers examination, diagnosis (including X-rays), advice on preventing problems, scale and polish (if clinically necessary), and application of fluoride varnish or fissure sealants.
- **Band 2 (£73.50)**: Covers everything in Band 1 PLUS extra treatment such as **fillings**, **root canal treatment**, and **tooth extractions**.
- **Band 3 (£319.10)**: Covers everything in Bands 1 and 2 PLUS complex lab work including **crowns**, **bridges**, **dentures**, and **inlays**.
- **Urgent Dental Care (£26.80)**: Covers emergency pain relief, temporary fillings, or urgent extractions at an NHS emergency dental clinic.

### 2. The Single Course of Treatment Rule
- Under NHS regulations, you pay **ONE single Band charge** for a completed course of treatment.
- *Crucial Rule*: If a dentist diagnoses that you need **4 fillings and 2 extractions**, you pay **ONE Band 2 charge of £73.50** for the entire course of treatment (NOT £73.50 per tooth).
- If additional treatment in the same or lower band is needed within **2 months** of completing a course, no further charge applies.

### 3. Statutory 100% Free NHS Dental Exemptions
You are legally EXEMPT from paying NHS dental charges (100% free treatment) if at the start of treatment you are:
- Aged **under 18** (or aged **under 19 and in full-time education**).
- **Pregnant** or have given birth in the **last 12 months** (requires a valid MatEx Maternity Exemption Certificate).
- Receiving qualifying income benefits: **Universal Credit** (with no income/take-home pay below threshold), **Income Support**, **Guarantee Credit element of Pension Credit**, or **income-based JSA / ESA**.
- Holder of an official **NHS HC2 Low Income Certificate**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & NHS Dental Warnings

1. **Falsely Claiming Free Dental Exemptions (NHS Penalty Notices)**: NHSBSA checks dental exemption claims against benefit databases. Incorrectly claiming free treatment results in a **£100 Penalty Charge Notice (PCN)** plus the original dental fee. Always verify your benefit entitlement before signing the FP17PR form.
2. **Confusing NHS Treatment with Private Upgrades**: NHS dentists must provide all treatment that is *clinically necessary* to maintain oral health. White composite fillings on back molar teeth or white crowns on rear teeth are classified as private cosmetic upgrades and billed at private rates.
3. **Failing to Accept a Written NHS Treatment Plan (FP17DC)**: For Band 2 or Band 3 treatment, your NHS dentist MUST provide a written personal treatment plan (Form FP17DC) detailing the NHS Band charge before starting treatment.

    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is the NHS Low Income Scheme (HC2 / HC3)? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The NHS Low Income Scheme provides financial help for healthcare costs. Applying via Form HC1 grants an HC2 certificate (100% free dental care, prescriptions, and sight tests) or HC3 certificate (partial help) based on household income.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Can an NHS dentist refuse to provide a scale and polish? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>A scale and polish is included in Band 1 only if your dentist decides it is *clinically necessary* to treat gum disease. If requested purely for cosmetic stain removal, your dentist may offer a private hygienist appointment.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How do I find an NHS dentist accepting new patients? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>

      <p>Use the official 'Find a dentist' tool on NHS.UK. Unlike GP surgeries, patients are not registered to a specific dental catchment area and can apply to any NHS dental practice in England.</p>
