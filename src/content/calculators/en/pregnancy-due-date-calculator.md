Calculating your estimated due date is one of the first exciting steps in pregnancy. In clinical practice, an average pregnancy lasts **280 days (40 weeks)** counted from the first day of your **Last Menstrual Period (LMP)**.

While only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date, calculating an accurate **Estimated Delivery Date (EDD)** helps structure antenatal care, schedule NHS ultrasound scans, and plan maternity leave.

## ⚙️ Clinical Calculation Formulas & Trimester Timelines

### 1. Naegele's Calculation Rule
The standard clinical rule for calculating your due date:
- **Formula**: `LMP Date + 1 Year - 3 Months + 7 Days` (adjusted for cycle length).
- **Cycle Adjustment**: For a standard 28-day cycle, ovulation occurs around day 14. If your cycle is 30 days, 2 days are added to the due date.

### 2. Trimester Milestone Windows
- **First Trimester (Weeks 1 to 12)**: Conception, heart development, and NHS 12-week dating scan (usually between weeks 11 and 14).
- **Second Trimester (Weeks 13 to 27)**: Rapid growth, movement detection (quickening), and NHS 20-week anomaly scan (weeks 18 to 21).
- **Third Trimester (Weeks 28 to 40+)**: Final weight gain, lung maturation, and preparing for labor.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Clinical Notes

1. **Confusing Gestational Age with Fetal Age**: Doctors measure pregnancy in **gestational age** (weeks since LMP), which includes the 2 weeks before conception actually occurred. Fetal age (actual age of the baby) is roughly 2 weeks shorter.
2. **Over-Relying on LMP over NHS Dating Scans**: Your 12-week NHS dating ultrasound scan provides a more precise measurement of crown-rump length (CRL). If there is a discrepancy of more than 5 days between your LMP due date and the scan date, midwife notes will update your EDD to match the scan.
3. **Worrying About Going Past Due Date**: A pregnancy is considered full term anywhere between **37 and 42 weeks**. Going past 40 weeks (overdue) is very common, especially in first pregnancies.

    <button class="faq-trigger">How does irregular menstrual cycle length affect my due date? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>If your menstrual cycle is longer than 28 days, ovulation happens later, pushing your due date back. If your cycle is shorter, ovulation occurs earlier. The NHS dating scan will accurately adjust for irregular cycles.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The 15th week before your expected due date (around week 25 of pregnancy) is your legal deadline to inform your employer in writing that you are pregnant and state when you plan to start your maternity leave.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">At how many weeks is a baby considered full term? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>A baby born between 37 weeks and 41 weeks + 6 days is considered full term. Early term is 37–38 weeks, full term is 39–40 weeks, and post-term is after 42 weeks.</p>
