Part-time employees in the UK pay Class 1 National Insurance Contributions (NICs) once their earnings cross statutory weekly and annual thresholds.

Understanding how the **Lower Earnings Limit (LEL)** and **Primary Threshold (PT)** interact allows part-time workers to optimize their hours, preserve State Pension qualifying years, and manage take-home pay.

## ⚙️ Statutory Class 1 NI Thresholds for 2026/27

### 1. Key National Insurance Thresholds
- **Lower Earnings Limit (LEL)**: **£123 per week** (£533/month; £6,396/year). Earnings above LEL give you a **qualifying year for State Pension** at zero NI tax cost.
- **Primary Threshold (PT)**: **£242 per week** (£1,047/month; **£12,570 per year**). You pay **0% NI** on earnings up to £242/week.
- **Upper Earnings Limit (UEL)**: **£967 per week** (£4,189/month; **£50,270 per year**).

### 2. Employee Class 1 NI Rates
- **Earnings up to £242/week (£12,570/yr)**: **0% NI**.
- **Earnings between £242/wk and £967/wk (£12,571 to £50,270/yr)**: **8% NI**.
- **Earnings above £967/week (£50,270/yr)**: **2% NI**.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Part-Time Traps

1. **Working Below £123/Week (Missing State Pension Credit)**: If you earn less than £123 per week (£6,396/year), you do not build up qualifying years for your UK State Pension. Working just enough hours to cross £123/week secures pension credit at 0% tax cost.
2. **Multiple Part-Time Jobs Threshold Rules**: National Insurance is calculated **per job, not per person**. If you work two part-time jobs earning £200/week each (£400/wk total), you pay £0 NI because neither individual job exceeds the £242/week threshold.
3. **Confusing NI Thresholds with Income Tax**: Income tax is calculated on your total aggregate income across all jobs, whereas NI is calculated separately on each individual payroll.

    <div class="faq-content">

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. If your part-time earnings fall below the Lower Earnings Limit (£123/week), you can make voluntary Class 3 National Insurance contributions (approx. £17.45/week) to plug gaps in your State Pension record.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
