Planning long-haul international air travel from UK airports involves calculating both **flight air duration** and **local time zone offsets**. Because flight schedules print departure and arrival times in the **local time** of each respective airport, calculating your true arrival time requires adding the flight duration and adjusting for time zone differences.

For example, a non-stop flight from London Heathrow (LHR) to New York JFK (JFK) takes approximately **7 hours and 30 minutes**. However, because New York is **5 hours behind London**, you land only **2 hours and 30 minutes** after your London departure time on the local New York clock.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Local Airport Time Rule**: Airlines ALWAYS display departure and arrival times in the **local standard or daylight saving time** of the originating and destination airports.
- **UK Time Zones**:
  - **Greenwich Mean Time (GMT = UTC+0)**: Active from the last Sunday in October to the last Sunday in March.
  - **British Summer Time (BST = UTC+1)**: Active from the last Sunday in March to the last Sunday in October.
- **Local Arrival Time Formula**:
  `Arrival Local Time = Departure Local Time + Flight Duration + (Destination UTC Offset - Origin UTC Offset)`.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Forgetting Daylight Savings Switching Dates**: The UK switches to BST on the last Sunday in March, whereas North America switches to Daylight Saving Time (EDT) 2 to 3 weeks earlier in March, creating a temporary 1-hour shift in time difference.
- **Confusing Flight Duration with Clock Difference**: A 14-hour eastbound flight from London to Tokyo (UTC+9) departs at 12:00 PM and lands at 11:00 AM the **next day** (+1 day shift) local Japanese time.
- **Miscalculating Layover Times**: When booking connecting flights, ensure layover times are calculated in local airport time to avoid missing connection transfers.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<div class="faq-sec">
  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Are flight schedule times shown in UK local time or destination local time?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Flight schedules, boarding passes, and e-tickets always list departure times in the local time of the departure airport, and arrival times in the local time of the destination airport.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How does British Summer Time (BST) affect international flight schedules?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>During British Summer Time (BST), which runs from late March to late October, the UK operates on UTC+1 (1 hour ahead of GMT). This reduces the time difference to New York from 5 hours to 4 hours during summer months.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is jet lag and how does time zone direction affect it?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Jet lag occurs when rapid travel across time zones disrupts your body's circadian clock. Traveling eastbound (e.g. UK to Asia) is generally harder to adapt to than traveling westbound (e.g. UK to USA) because extending your day is easier for the body than shortening it.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What compensation rights apply if a UK flight is delayed?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Under UK Regulation UK261, if your flight departing a UK airport arrives at your destination 3 or more hours late due to airline fault, you are entitled to statutory cash compensation ranging between £220 and £520 per passenger depending on flight distance.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
