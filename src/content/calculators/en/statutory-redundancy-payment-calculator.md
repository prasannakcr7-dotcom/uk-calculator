If you are made redundant in the UK after completing **2 or more years of continuous service** with your employer, you are legally entitled to receive **Statutory Redundancy Pay (SRP)**.

Your statutory redundancy payment entitlement is calculated using a statutory formula based on your **age**, **full completed years of service** (capped at a maximum of 20 years), and your **gross weekly pay** (capped at the statutory maximum weekly cap set annually by the UK government).

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Statutory Redundancy Entitlement Formula**:
  - **Age 41 and older**: **1.5 weeks' pay** for each full year of service.
  - **Age 22 to 40**: **1.0 week's pay** for each full year of service.
  - **Under age 22**: **0.5 week's pay** for each full year of service.
- **Statutory Limits & Caps (2026/27)**:
  - **Maximum Statutory Weekly Pay Cap**: **£700 per week** (if your gross weekly pay is higher, it is capped at £700/wk).
  - **Maximum Length of Service**: Capped at **20 completed years**.
  - **Maximum Statutory Redundancy Award**: **£21,000** (20 years × 1.5 × £700).
- **HMRC £30,000 Tax Exemption**: Statutory redundancy payments up to **£30,000 are 100% tax-free** and exempt from National Insurance. Any severance pay exceeding £30,000 is subject to Income Tax and employer NI.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Forgetting That Contractual Redundancy Pay May Be Higher**: Many UK employers offer enhanced contractual redundancy pay (e.g. 1 month's pay per year of service) which exceeds statutory minimums.
- **Counting Partial Years of Service**: Statutory redundancy pay counts full completed years of service only; 4 years and 11 months of service counts as 4 completed years.
- **Failing to Claim Statutory Notice Pay**: Redundancy pay is paid *in addition* to your statutory or contractual notice period pay (or Payment in Lieu of Notice - PILON).
