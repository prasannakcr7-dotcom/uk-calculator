Designing underground foul and surface water pipework in the UK requires calculating the correct **drainage gradient (fall)** under **Building Regulations Approved Document H**.

If a pipe gradient is **too shallow**, liquids move too slowly and fail to flush solid waste away. If a gradient is **too steep**, water drains rapidly leaving heavy solids behind, causing severe pipe blockages. Setting a **1 in 40 (25mm drop per metre)** or **1 in 80 (12.5mm drop per metre)** gradient maintains a **self-cleansing velocity**.

## ⚙️ Statutory UK Drainage Rules & Gradients for 2026/27

### 1. Drainage Fall & Gradient Formulas
- **Gradient Ratio**: Expressed as **1 in X** (meaning 1 unit of vertical drop for every X units of horizontal pipe run).
- **Total Vertical Drop Formula**: `Total Drop (mm) = [ Pipe Length (m) ÷ Gradient Ratio X ] × 1,000`.
- **Drop per Metre Formula**: `Drop per Metre (mm/m) = 1,000 ÷ Gradient Ratio X`.
- *Example*: A **12.0 metre run** of 110mm pipe laid at a **1 in 40 gradient**:
  - `Drop per Metre = 1,000 ÷ 40 = 25.0 mm per metre`.
  - `Total Drop = (12.0 ÷ 40) × 1,000 = 300 mm (0.30 metres)`.

### 2. Approved Document H Gradient Specifications
- **110mm Foul Drainage Pipes (Standard Domestic)**:
  - **Standard Gradient**: **1 in 40** (25mm drop per metre / 2.5% slope).
  - **Minimum Gradient (with 1 WC connected)**: **1 in 40**.
  - **Minimum Gradient (with 5+ WCs connected)**: **1 in 80** (12.5mm drop per metre / 1.25% slope).
- **150mm Main Sewer Pipes**:
  - **Minimum Gradient**: **1 in 150** (6.7mm drop per metre) provided peak flow is adequate.

### 3. Statutory Self-Cleansing Velocity (0.7 m/s)
- Approved Document H requires wastewater to travel at a minimum **self-cleansing velocity of 0.7 metres per second (m/s)** to ensure paper and solid waste are carried cleanly along the pipe.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Drainage Warnings

1. **Laying Pipes Steeper Than 1 in 20**: Gradients steeper than 1 in 20 (5% slope) cause water to rush ahead of solid waste, leaving paper and faeces deposited on pipe walls, leading to recurring drain blockages.
2. **Pipe Sagging (Reverse Gradients)**: Underground pipes must be supported on a 100mm bed of **pea shingle aggregate (10mm single-size)**. Failing to bed pipes correctly causes soil settlement, creating sags (belly drops) where standing water collects.
3. **Inspection Chamber Spacing (Manholes)**: Under Building Regs, inspection chambers / manholes MUST be installed at every change of pipe direction, change of pipe size, or at maximum **30-metre intervals** along straight pipe runs.
