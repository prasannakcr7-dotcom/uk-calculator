In the UK higher education system, undergraduate bachelor's degrees with honours (BA, BSc, BEng, LLB) are awarded with a final **Degree Classification** based on your overall weighted average percentage mark achieved across your Second Year (Level 5) and Final Year (Level 6) modules. First Year (Level 4) marks count toward credit progression but rarely contribute to your final degree classification.

UK universities enforce formal **Borderline Upgrade Rules**. If your overall weighted average falls within 2 percentage points of a higher classification boundary (for example, **68.0% to 69.9%** between a 2:1 and a 1st), exam boards apply statutory algorithm rules—such as "exit velocity" (majority of Final Year credits in the higher band) or dissertation performance—to determine whether to upgrade your degree.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **UK Degree Classification Bands**:
  - **First Class Honours (1st)**: **70.0% to 100%**.
  - **Upper Second Class Honours (2:1)**: **60.0% to 69.9%**.
  - **Lower Second Class Honours (2:2)**: **50.0% to 59.9%**.
  - **Third Class Honours (3rd)**: **40.0% to 49.9%**.
  - **Fail / Non-Honours Pass**: **Below 40.0%**.
- **Standard Year Weighting Ratios**:
  - **1:2 Ratio (Most Common)**: Year 2 counts for **33.3%** and Final Year counts for **66.7%**.
  - **1:4 Ratio**: Year 2 counts for **20%** and Final Year counts for **80%**.
  - **50/50 Equal Weighting**: Year 2 counts for **50%** and Final Year counts for **50%**.
- **Borderline Upgrade Conditions (68%-69.9% or 58%-59.9%)**: Most UK universities upgrade a student sitting in a 2% borderline zone if at least 50% of Final Year credits (60 out of 120 credits) are in the higher classification band, or if the dissertation mark achieves the higher classification.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Assuming First Year (Level 4) Marks Count**: In standard 3-year UK degrees, Year 1 marks count 0% toward your final degree classification.
- **Overlooking Credit Weighting of Modules**: A 30-credit dissertation module carries twice the weight of a 15-credit standard module in your Final Year average.
- **Ignoring University Borderline Criteria**: Different UK universities use different borderline rules; check your university's specific Academic Regulations handbook for exact exit velocity rules.
