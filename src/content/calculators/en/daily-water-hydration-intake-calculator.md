Staying adequately hydrated is essential for regulating body temperature, maintaining cognitive focus, promoting joint lubrication, supporting kidney function, and preventing headaches.

The **NHS Eatwell Guide** recommends that adults in the UK consume **6 to 8 glasses of fluid per day** (approx. **1.5 to 2.0 litres**). However, your true individual fluid requirement depends dynamically on your **body weight**, **physical exercise duration**, and **ambient environmental temperature**.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Baseline Water Calculation Formula**:
  `Baseline Fluid Target (Litres) = Body Weight in kg × 0.035`.
- **Exercise Fluid Compensation**: Add approx. **350 ml (0.35 L)** for every 30 minutes of moderate-to-intense physical exercise.
- **Climate Temperature Factor**: Increase fluid target by **15%** during hot summer weather (25°C+) or in heated indoor environments.
- **Eligible Fluids Under NHS Guidelines**:
  - Water (ideal zero-calorie hydration).
  - Plain tea and coffee (without added sugar).
  - Lower-fat milk.
  - Fruit juices and smoothies (limited to 150ml daily due to free sugars).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Waiting Until You Feel Thirsty**: Thirst is a late indicator of dehydration; by the time you feel thirsty, your body is already 1% to 2% dehydrated.
- **Counting Sugary Carbonated Drinks**: Hydrating with high-sugar energy or soft drinks adds empty calories and can cause blood sugar spikes.
- **Over-hydrating Excessively**: Drinking excessive amounts of water (> 5–6 litres/day) without electrolyte replenishment can lead to hyponatremia (dangerously low blood sodium levels).

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Common signs of dehydration include dark yellow or strong-smelling urine, feeling thirsty, dizziness, dry mouth, tiredness, and headaches. Clear or pale straw-coloured urine indicates good hydration.</p>

  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How much extra water should I drink when exercising?</button>
