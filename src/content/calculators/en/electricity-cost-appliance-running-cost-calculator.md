With energy prices remaining a key household expense in the UK, understanding the **exact running cost of individual household electrical appliances**—from 3,000W electric fan heaters and tumble dryers to 7,000W EV home chargers—helps consumers reduce electricity bills.

Electricity in the UK is billed per **Kilowatt-hour (kWh)**—commonly referred to as a "unit" of electricity. One kWh represents using 1,000 Watts of electrical power continuously for 1 hour.

## ⚙️ Rules & Thresholds

- **Appliance Energy Consumption Formula**:
  `Daily Energy (kWh) = (Appliance Wattage Rating in Watts × Hours Used Per Day) / 1,000`.
- **Running Cost Calculation**:
  `Daily Cost (£) = Daily kWh × (Electricity Unit Rate in Pence / 100)`.
- **Ofgem National Price Cap Benchmarks (2026/27)**:
  - Default Electricity Unit Rate Cap: **~24.50p per kWh** (varies slightly by region).
  - Daily Electricity Standing Charge Cap: **~60.10p per day**.
- **Common UK Appliance Wattages**:
  - **Electric Fan Heater**: 2,000W – 3,000W (approx. 49p – 74p per hour).
  - **Tumble Dryer (Vented/Condenser)**: 2,500W (approx. 61p per hour).
  - **Tumble Dryer (Heat Pump)**: 900W (approx. 22p per hour).
  - **Air Fryer**: 1,500W (approx. 37p per hour).
  - **Plasma TV / Gaming PC**: 250W (approx. 6p per hour).
  - **LED Light Bulb**: 9W (approx. 0.22p per hour).

## 📑 Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing Peak Power Rating with Thermostatic Average**: Refrigerator compressors or heat pump dryers cycle on and off thermostatically, consuming less average power than their maximum peak wattage sticker rating.
- **Forgetting Daily Standing Charges**: The daily standing charge (~60p/day) is charged regardless of how much electricity you consume.
- **Using Outdated Price Cap Unit Rates**: Comparing appliance costs using 2022 peak crisis rates (34p/kWh) overstates running costs compared to current 2026/27 price cap levels (~24.5p/kWh).

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is a Kilowatt-hour (kWh) in UK electricity billing?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>A Kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the standard metric unit of energy used by UK energy suppliers to bill electricity consumption. It equals using 1,000 Watts of power for 1 hour (or 100 Watts for 10 hours).</p>
    </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How much does an air fryer cost to run compared to a conventional oven?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>A typical 1,500W air fryer running for 20 minutes consumes 0.5 kWh (costing ~12p). A 2,500W conventional electric oven running for 45 minutes consumes 1.875 kWh (costing ~46p). Air fryers save money primarily due to shorter cooking times.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What is Economy 7 and how does off-peak electricity pricing work?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Economy 7 is a UK differential electricity tariff providing 7 hours of cheaper off-peak electricity overnight (often ~12p/kWh) for storage heaters and EV charging, balanced by a higher daytime peak unit rate (~30p/kWh).</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Are heat pump tumble dryers cheaper to run than condenser dryers?</button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Heat pump tumble dryers recycle hot air rather than venting it, drawing only ~900 Watts compared to ~2,500 Watts for standard condenser dryers. This reduces drying cycle running costs by over 50%.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
