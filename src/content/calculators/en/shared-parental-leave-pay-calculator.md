Shared Parental Leave (SPL) and Statutory Shared Parental Pay (ShPP) enable eligible working parents to share up to **50 weeks of leave** and up to **37 weeks of pay** between them after having a baby or adopting a child.

This system allows parents to take leave together, alternate blocks of leave, or customize child care responsibilities during their baby's first year.

## ⚙️ Statutory ShPP Rates & Allocation Rules for 2026/27

### 1. Leave & Pay Pools
- **Shared Leave Pool**: Up to **50 weeks** of leave can be shared (52 weeks total statutory maternity leave minus 2 compulsory maternity weeks taken by mother).
- **Shared Pay Pool**: Up to **37 weeks** of Statutory Shared Parental Pay (ShPP) can be shared (39 weeks total statutory maternity pay minus 2 weeks taken by mother).

### 2. Statutory ShPP Rate
- **Weekly Flat Rate**: Paid at **£184.03 per week**, or **90% of average gross weekly earnings** (whichever amount is lower).

### 3. Eligibility Criteria
Both parents must meet specific continuity of employment and earnings tests:
- **Continuity Test**: Worked for the same employer for at least **26 weeks** by the 15th week before the baby is due.
- **Earnings Test**: Earned at least **£123.00 per week** on average during 8 reference weeks.

## 📑 Common Pitfalls & Employer Notices

1. **Missing the 8-Week Booking Notice Window**: Parents must give their respective employers at least **8 weeks' written notice** before starting a block of Shared Parental Leave.
2. **Confusing Continuous vs. Discontinuous Leave**: Employers must agree to continuous leave requests (e.g. 12 straight weeks off). However, if you request discontinuous leave (e.g. 2 weeks on, 2 weeks off), your employer has the legal right to refuse.
3. **Forgetting PAYE Deductions**: Statutory Shared Parental Pay is treated as normal taxable income and is subject to PAYE Income Tax and Class 1 National Insurance.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Both parents can take Shared Parental Leave at the same time, allowing you to spend weeks off together as a family, provided the total combined leave taken does not exceed the available 50-week pool.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">How does a mother curtail her maternity leave to start SPL? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>The mother must submit a formal 'Maternity Leave Curtailment Notice' to her employer, specifying the exact date her maternity leave and pay will end so the remaining weeks transfer into the shared parental pool.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">Are there SPLIT (Shared Parental Leave in Touch) days? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Yes. Each parent taking Shared Parental Leave is entitled to up to 20 SPLIT days (in addition to the mother's 10 KIT days). These allow you to work occasionally without breaking your leave contract.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="faq-item">
    <button class="faq-trigger">What if one parent is self-employed? <span class="icon">▼</span></button>
    <div class="faq-content">
      <p>Self-employed parents cannot take Shared Parental Leave or receive ShPP. However, if one parent is self-employed and meets the Employment and Earnings test, their employed partner may still qualify to take SPL and ShPP.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
